\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:mp_discussion}

The evidence supports a bounded statement.  In a declared ordered-profile
representation and finite dictionary, replicated source tasks can learn a
useful response-function structure.  A small common target design can then
improve early mean prediction, and target evidence can reduce the weight of a
mismatched structure.  The paired full-dictionary control shows that this
effect is attributable to source-selected function structure on the checked
development block.

The evidence does not support three stronger statements.  First, observable
exposures are not a learned trajectory representation; earlier encoder and
state-coordinate gates did not produce a transferable end-to-end gain.
Second, improved total log-risk prediction did not yield a risk-specific
deployment gain, and coefficient recovery was not improved.  Third, the
independent deployment gate was not passed: only 3/8 source-structure tasks
improved, while the learned-risk arm had a small negative mean difference.
The generic target-only RBF-KG control also exceeded the source-selected arm by
0.00112 on development and 0.01638 on v2, so the source structure is not a
generic constrained-BO improvement under this contract.

The task law is synthetic and the candidate bank can miss the safe region.
The feasible-deployment range was declared before the development block.  The
original 95433--95440 confirmation interval was not run as a block, but seed
95434 overlaps the development artifact and is not eligible as untouched
confirmation.  The separate v2 block retained all eight new seeds but did not
reproduce the development ordering.  The source
archive is assumed to be available; retrieval cost and operational value are
not solved.  The finite dictionary is not an arbitrary high-dimensional
optimizer, and the legacy atlas and Energy results answer a different
initial-design question.  Finally, zero false certificates in these cells is
evidence about the declared verifier and replication law, not a universal
safety guarantee.
