\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Experiments Identify}

The central empirical finding is an attribution result.  Under a target budget
of 13 calls, the structural frontend determines whether the search observes a
feasible basin; the online backend determines a smaller, conditional objective
improvement after coverage.  This distinction explains why several transfer
baselines become strong when supplied with the same frozen atlas and why a
powerful high-dimensional optimizer can fail under common Sobol.  The proposed
object is therefore not ``SAASBO plus a warm start.''  It is a reusable,
source-trained experimental design whose value can be measured with the
backend held fixed.

The component ablation also refines the structural story.  A universal low-
frequency library alone is too generic, and source templates alone are too
brittle.  Their union, followed by source-risk/objective ranking and maximin
coverage, is robust across the three synthetic mechanisms.  The external
energy audit then reveals an important boundary: in a domain with an obvious
constant-profile control, a natural low-frequency grid is as good as the
source-trained atlas.  Source learning is valuable when it identifies a
nontrivial transferable profile family, not merely because historical data
exist.

\subsection{Operational Meaning of the Cost Decomposition}

The method trades reusable offline cost and explicit verification cost for a
small target search budget.  In a one-off target problem, $S=384$ source calls
must be counted in full.  In a repeated deployment program, the same archive
can support many target tasks and its per-target cost decreases, but this
amortization is an operational decision rather than a statistical free pass.
Likewise, independent verification often costs far more than the 13 search
calls.  That is not a defect hidden by the analysis: it is the price of a
deployment statement that does not trust the optimizer's posterior.

This decomposition is useful for managers choosing among protocols.  If the
decision is exploratory and no deployment certificate is required, proposal-
only or atlas-plus-Sobol may be sufficient.  If objective quality matters after
coverage, canonical SAASBO adds value at higher computation cost.  If an unsafe
deployment has material consequences, verification should be budgeted as a
separate final experiment rather than borrowed from search data.

\subsection{Why the High-Dimensional Result Is Plausible}

The raw search spaces are enormous, but the benchmark policies affect system
behavior through coarse profile summaries.  The source archive identifies
which low-frequency profiles should be transferred, and the inverse map
constructs those profiles at a new dimension.  The relevant statistical
dimension is therefore the geometry of $\etaHat$, not the number of integer
entries.  Theorem \ref{thm:coverage} captures precisely this mechanism.

This mechanism is also falsifiable.  If target safety depends on isolated
high-frequency coordinates, if source and target safe supports are far apart,
or if coordinate error consumes the safe depth, condition
\eqref{eq:coverage-condition} fails.  The finite-atlas no-free-lunch theorem
then applies.  We regard that explicit failure mode as preferable to an opaque
claim of universal meta-learning.

\subsection{Heteroscedasticity and Certification}

The HVD diagnostic shows that variance structure can be statistically
recoverable without changing the selected policy.  This is unsurprising when
the atlas already places a deep feasible point in the initial design and the
terminal verifier can spend more samples.  The decomposition may matter in
applications where shared shocks dominate, safe depth is narrow, or
verification is more expensive; the present data do not establish such an
optimization gain.  We therefore keep Equation \eqref{eq:hvd} and its formal
properties as a secondary risk-modeling result, not as a reason to enlarge the
paper's main claim.

\subsection{Limitations}

Five limitations delimit external validity.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The three primary domains are synthetic and intentionally satisfy a
related structural family.  Their different operations interpretations do not
replace independent real systems.
\item The energy study contains one untouched market-year target.  It supports
low-frequency structural compression but not superiority to a natural
structured control.
\item The global margin Lipschitz constant and ideal-coordinate error are
assumptions.  The registered finite-library audit passes in all three domains,
but a global certificate is unavailable.
\item Verification controls its declared statistical event under fresh-sample
independence.  Distribution shift after deployment is outside that contract.
\item The source archive is fixed at two domains and 384 calls.  Richer task
collections could improve or destabilize the atlas; learning curves in the
number and diversity of source tasks remain future work.
\end{enumerate}

The failed traffic development gate is retained in the experiment registry and
supports the same caution: not every domain exposes a transferable feasible
profile at the chosen fidelity.  We do not use that failed development study as
positive evidence in this paper.
