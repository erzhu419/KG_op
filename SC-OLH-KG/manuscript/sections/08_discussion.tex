\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence supports}

The study supports a conditional proposition: when target feasibility is
organized by profile geometry represented in the source population, source
outcomes can improve a very small structural initial design.  The randomized
task law tests more than the three mechanisms used during method development,
and its permutation, rank, piecewise, frequency-shift, and misspecification
regimes make the condition falsifiable.  Source scoring improves aggregate
coverage, yet the regime table and Energy study identify concrete reversals.

This interpretation differs from claiming a generic high-dimensional
optimizer.  The raw vector may contain $10^4$ coordinates, but the decision is
an ordered profile and the library has finite structural complexity.  Profile
grid refinement is operationally relevant---a controller may require a fine
lookup table---but it is not equivalent to adding $10^4$ unrelated decision
variables.  The proper benefit is invariance to representation resolution
under shared profile structure.

\subsection{Why Energy is necessary}

Removing Energy would make the evidence cleaner and the scientific claim
weaker.  The external reversal shows that a generic structural design can be
better than source scoring when region-held-out source rankings do not transfer.
Because V3 was frozen before its outcomes, tuning the source score after this
result would convert the holdout into development data.  We therefore retain
Energy as a negative control and interpret it as an applicability boundary.

The practical implication is a diagnostic choice.  If historical tasks and a
new target share an ordered policy semantics and source rank stability can be
validated on genuinely held-out tasks, source scoring is reasonable.  If the
target is temporally or geographically shifted and generic low-frequency
structure already has a credible physical basis, the generic maximin design is
the safer default.  The method should abstain from a transfer claim when these
conditions cannot be assessed.

\subsection{Source cost as a portfolio investment}

The equal-preverification-cost experiment prevents an appealing but incorrect
story: 10 target calls do not beat 394 target calls for a single target.  A
source archive becomes economically plausible only when reused.  Let
$C_0=N_0+V_0$ be the expected target-only calls per deployment and
$C_A=N_A+V_A$ the source-design calls excluding the archive.  The call-count
break-even number of deployments is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:break-even}
 M_{\rm break}=\left\lceil\frac{S}{C_0-C_A}\right\rceil,
 \qquad C_0>C_A.
\end{equation}
In the randomized primary experiment, mean-call estimates give thresholds of
about 7, 9, and 11 targets relative to generic DCT, natural structure, and raw
Sobol.  This arithmetic does not include differences in simulator setup,
wall-clock parallelism, or policy quality, and Energy shows that paying the
archive can still be inferior.  It is a planning threshold, not a dominance
theorem.

\subsection{Verification is a separate operational decision}

Independent verification frequently costs an order of magnitude more than
target search.  That is not overhead hidden by the method; it is the cost of a
different claim.  Search asks whether a promising policy has been found.
Verification asks whether a frozen policy can be deployed with a specified
false-certification bound.  A target-only optimizer making the same safety
claim must pay a comparable fresh-sample cost.

The all-success protocol is particularly conservative near the threshold.
Its low power explains why feasible coverage greatly exceeds certified
deployment.  Increasing an all-success budget can even reduce power for any
$p<1$.  Future deployments should preregister a more efficient exact
Clopper--Pearson or sequential ranking-and-selection design rather than change
the rule after inspecting results.  Temporal applications also require block
or mixing-aware certification if iid replications are not physically
defensible.

\subsection{Benchmark fit and remaining threats}

The frozen randomized generator reduces development overfitting but cannot
erase it.  The task law, profile library, and stress axes were authored within
the project.  The method also assumes declared ordered semantics, and the
schema-blind drop confirms that this is a strong prior.  Energy is genuinely
different and unfavorable, but five geographic regions are too few for a broad
population claim.  The native transfer audit has only three historical
families and all methods fail on Queue.

Other limitations are equally important.  Three source replications cannot
reliably order near-tied profiles; source volume is nonmonotone; the finite
library can miss a feasible basin entirely; and source rank aggregation can
hide task disagreement.  Objective comparisons among certified subsets are
selection dependent.  The randomized oracle measures regret relative to a
finite audit library, not the unknown continuous optimum.  One canonical
functional-SCBO task failed and remains in the denominator.  Finally, the
formal theory proves finite implications conditional on alignment and iid
assumptions; it does not prove those assumptions from data.

These boundaries argue for a restrained deployment workflow: preregister the
library and task law, keep a source-free structured control, validate rank
stability on independent tasks, price source and verification calls, and
retain abstention.  Under those conditions, historical simulation is a
transparent experimental-design asset rather than a hidden oracle.
