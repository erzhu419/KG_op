\section{Finite-Sample Guarantees and Scope}\label{sec:theory}

The theory explains the finite implementation under explicit conditions.  It
does not assert that every target is aligned with the source population.

\subsection{Profile-grid consistency}

Let $c_k(h)=\int_0^1 h(t)b_k(t)\,dt$, where $b_0(t)=1$ and
$b_k(t)=\sqrt2\cos(\pi kt)$ for $k\ge1$.  Let $\widehat h_d$ be a reconstruction
from $d$ ordered samples.

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient stability]\label{prop:coefficient-stability}
If $\|h-\widehat h_d\|_\infty\le\epsilon_d$ and
$\|b_k\|_\infty\le B_k$, then
\[
 |c_k(h)-c_k(\widehat h_d)|\le \epsilon_d B_k.
\]
If $h$ is $L_h$-Lipschitz and $\widehat h_d$ is the regular-midpoint
piecewise-constant reconstruction, then $\epsilon_d\le L_h/(2d)$.
Consequently the error of the frequency-penalized coefficient is at most
\[
 \frac{L_hB_k}{2d(1+\kappa k)}.
\]
Linear interpolation from a reference grid of size $d_{\rm ref}$ has the
analogous bound $L_h/(2d_{\rm ref})$ before deterministic rounding.
\end{proposition}

The proof applies the integral triangle inequality and the nearest-node or
linear-interpolation error.  It justifies comparison across discretizations of
one regular profile; it does not turn an unordered vector into a profile.

\subsection{Finite-library coverage}

For any coordinate $\eta$ on the finite library, define the covering radius of
$A\subset\cL$ by
\[
 r(A;\eta)=\max_{h\in\cL}\min_{a\in A}\|\eta(h)-\eta(a)\|_2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Farthest-first finite $k$-center bound]
\label{thm:farthest-first}
Let $A_k$ be the $k$ centers produced by Gonzalez farthest-first from any
declared first center, and let $r_k^\star$ be the smallest covering radius
attainable by any $k$ library members.  Then
\[
 r(A_k;\eta)\le 2r_k^\star.
\]
\end{theorem}

The standard witness proof uses the $k$ centers and one farthest unselected
point.  Two of these $k+1$ pairwise-separated witnesses must share an optimal
cluster.  The implementation emits this witness certificate for every design.

\subsection{Source-score uncertainty}

Let $s_{qj}$ denote either a true source objective statistic or a true source
chance-margin statistic and $\widehat s_{qj}$ its replicated estimate.

\begin{proposition}[Separated-rank recovery]\label{prop:rank-recovery}
Suppose $|\widehat s_{qj}-s_{qj}|\le r_q$ simultaneously over a finite source
library.  Every pair satisfying $|s_{qj}-s_{q\ell}|>2r_q$ has the correct
estimated order.  If the replicated mean errors are independent sub-Gaussian
with proxies $\sigma_{qjr}$, then a union bound over tasks, profiles, and the
two heads gives an explicit probability for the simultaneous mean-error
event.  The same conclusion applies to the chance margin when the sample-scale
error is bounded separately.
\end{proposition}

This result does not promise exact recovery of nearly tied ranks from three
replications.  The sensitivity experiment therefore varies replications and
library size rather than treating the source order as known.

\subsection{Conditional source-to-target coverage}

Let $m_q(h)$ be the target chance margin, with $m_q(h)\le0$ implying
feasibility.  Let $\eta_q^\star$ be an ideal task coordinate and $\widehat\eta$
the implemented coordinate.  The following statement exposes the assumption
that source and target share useful profile geometry.

\begin{assumption}[Aligned profile geometry]\label{ass:alignment}
There exist a target safe-center profile $h_q^\star$, a library member $h_s$,
and constants $\gamma_q,\Delta_q,\epsilon_\eta,L_q\ge0$ such that
\begin{align*}
 m_q(h_q^\star)&\le-\gamma_q,\\
 \|\eta_q^\star(h_s)-\eta_q^\star(h_q^\star)\|_2&\le\Delta_q,\\
 \|\widehat\eta(h)-\eta_q^\star(h)\|_2&\le\epsilon_\eta
 \quad\text{for the profiles used below},\\
 |m_q(h)-m_q(h')|&\le L_q
 \|\eta_q^\star(h)-\eta_q^\star(h')\|_2.
\end{align*}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Aligned atlas coverage]\label{thm:aligned-coverage}
Let $\atlas$ cover $\cL$ within radius $r_{n_0}$ in $\widehat\eta$.  Under
Assumption \ref{ass:alignment}, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage-condition}
 L_q\bigl(r_{n_0}+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta\bigr)\le\gamma_q,
\end{equation}
then $\atlas$ contains a target-feasible policy.
\end{theorem}

The proof chooses the atlas member covering $h_s$, transfers the distance to
$\eta_q^\star$ by two coordinate-error terms, and applies Lipschitz margin and
safe depth.  Equation \eqref{eq:coverage-condition} is useful precisely because
it can fail: frequency shifts, irregular grids, and misspecified targets raise
$\Delta_q$ or $\epsilon_\eta$, while shallow feasible basins reduce
$\gamma_q$.

For a fixed atlas and independent held-out tasks $Q_1,\ldots,Q_T$ from a
declared task law, let $H_t$ indicate whether the atlas contains a feasible
policy.  Standard bounded-variable concentration gives, with probability at
least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:task-law-bound}
 \E_{Q\sim\cQ}[H(Q)]
 \ge \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T H_t
      -\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2T}}.
\end{equation}
The probability refers only to the registered task law and requires the atlas
to be frozen before the held-out tasks.  It is not a guarantee for arbitrary
future domains.

\subsection{Exact terminal verification}

Fix a shortlist before verification.  Candidate $j$ receives $v_j$ fresh iid
Bernoulli indicators $Z_{jr}=1\{Y^g_{jr}\le\tau\}$ and is certified only when
$\sum_rZ_{jr}=v_j$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-success-test}
 p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j,
 \qquad p_0=1-\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $\delta_j=\delta/K_s$ in the primary protocol.

\begin{theorem}[Candidate and familywise validity]\label{thm:verifier}
For any fixed unsafe candidate with true feasibility probability $p_j<p_0$,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{candidate $j$ is certified}\}=p_j^{v_j}
 \le p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j.
\]
For a frozen shortlist, regardless of dependence among the candidate tests,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{an unsafe policy is deployed}\}
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{K_s}\delta_j\le\delta.
\]
Sequentially stopping at the first certificate does not alter this union-bound
argument because the shortlist and all budgets are fixed before testing.
\end{theorem}

For $p_0=.95$, $K_s=3$, and $\delta=.05$, the minimum all-success budget is 80
per candidate.  Its power at true feasibility probability $p$ is $p^{80}$;
this is only 0.017 at $p=.95$.  Validity therefore does not imply
nonvacuity, and Section \ref{sec:results} reports both.  The exact
Clopper--Pearson lower bound is used only for the preregistered sensitivity
calculation \citep{ClopperPearson1934}.

\subsection{Formalization boundary}

The profile consistency, finite $k$-center, rank-recovery, aligned-coverage,
task-law, binomial, and budget implications are formalized in Lean 4 without
\texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, or project-defined \texttt{axiom}.  Machine
checking establishes that the finite conclusions follow from their premises.
It does not establish that Assumption \ref{ass:alignment} holds in Energy or
any unspecified target population; those questions are empirical and the
negative experiments are retained.
