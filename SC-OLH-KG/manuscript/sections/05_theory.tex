\section{Why the Design Can Work: Finite-Sample Guarantees}\label{sec:theory}

The method uses only ten target policies, so its structural assumption must do
real work.  The results below trace that work from representation to
deployment.  Proposition \ref{prop:coefficient-stability} first shows that a
policy has nearly the same coordinate when the implementation grid is
refined.  Theorem \ref{thm:farthest-first} then shows that farthest-first gives
a controlled cover of the finite library.  Proposition
\ref{prop:rank-recovery} states which source rankings survive finite
simulation noise.  Theorem \ref{thm:aligned-coverage} turns structural cover
into a feasible target policy when a source-supported profile lies close
enough to a sufficiently deep target safe region.  Finally, Theorem
\ref{thm:verifier} controls false deployment using fresh target simulations.

This chain also makes the limitation visible.  A fine grid does not hurt if it
describes the same ordered profile, but no theorem can rescue a target whose
safe region is absent from, or badly distorted relative to, the structural
library.  The results are finite and conditional; they are not a guarantee for
arbitrary high-dimensional black boxes.

\subsection{Profile-grid consistency}

Let $c_k(h)=\int_0^1 h(t)b_k(t)\,dt$, where $b_0(t)=1$ and
$b_k(t)=\sqrt2\cos(\pi kt)$ for $k\ge1$.  Let $\widehat h_d$ be a reconstruction
from $d$ ordered samples.

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient stability]\label{prop:coefficient-stability}
If $\|h-\widehat h_d\|_\infty\le\epsilon_d$ and
$\|b_k\|_\infty\le B_k$, then
\[
 |c_k(h)-c_k(\widehat h_d)|\le \epsilon_d B_k.
\]
If $h$ is $L_h$-Lipschitz and $\widehat h_d$ is the regular-midpoint
piecewise-constant reconstruction, then $\epsilon_d\le L_h/(2d)$.
Consequently the error of the frequency-penalized coefficient is at most
\[
 \frac{L_hB_k}{2d(1+\kappa k)}.
\]
Linear interpolation from a reference grid of size $d_{\rm ref}$ has the
analogous bound $L_h/(2d_{\rm ref})$ before deterministic rounding.
\end{proposition}

The bound says that nominal dimension disappears once the grid is fine enough
to reconstruct the same profile.  It justifies comparisons across
discretizations of one ordered rule; it does not turn an unordered vector into
a function.  The proof is the integral triangle inequality followed by the
nearest-node or linear-interpolation error.

\subsection{Why ten diverse profiles cover the library}

For any coordinate $u$ on the finite library, define the covering radius of
$A\subset\cL$ by
\[
 r(A;u)=\max_{h\in\cL}\min_{a\in A}\|u(h)-u(a)\|_2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Farthest-first finite $k$-center bound]
\label{thm:farthest-first}
Let $A_k$ be the $k$ centers produced by Gonzalez farthest-first from any
declared first center, and let $r_k^\star$ be the smallest covering radius
attainable by any $k$ library members.  Then
\[
 r(A_k;u)\le 2r_k^\star.
\]
\end{theorem}

The standard witness proof uses the $k$ centers and one farthest unselected
point.  Two of these $k+1$ pairwise-separated witnesses must share an optimal
cluster.  The implementation emits this witness certificate for every design.
For the implemented $\eta=(z,r^g,r^f)$, Euclidean projection gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-cover}
 r(A_k;z)\le r(A_k;\eta)\le2r_k^\star(\eta).
\end{equation}
Thus source ranks can steer selection without erasing structural diversity.
Equation \eqref{eq:projection-cover} gives a checkable cover in profile space;
it does not claim that source ranks have the same meaning on the target.

\subsection{What finite source samples can rank}

Let $s_{qj}$ denote either a true source objective statistic or a true source
chance-margin statistic and $\widehat s_{qj}$ its replicated estimate.

\begin{proposition}[Separated-rank recovery]\label{prop:rank-recovery}
Suppose $|\widehat s_{qj}-s_{qj}|\le r_q$ simultaneously over a finite source
library.  Every pair satisfying $|s_{qj}-s_{q\ell}|>2r_q$ has the correct
estimated order.
\end{proposition}

For the Gaussian simulator used in the registered synthetic study, let
$R\ge2$, $\nu=R-1$, and let $q_{\nu,p}$ be the $p$ quantile of
$\chi^2_\nu$.  For one profile with true mean $\mu$ and scale $\sigma$, define
\begin{align}
 r_\mu(\delta_\mu)
   &=\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta_\mu)}{R}},\\
 a_\nu(\delta)&=1-\sqrt{q_{\nu,\delta/2}/\nu},\\
 b_\nu(\delta)&=\sqrt{q_{\nu,1-\delta/2}/\nu}-1,\\
 r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma)
   &=\sigma\max\{a_\nu(\delta_\sigma),b_\nu(\delta_\sigma)\}.
\end{align}
The normal mean tail and Cochran identity
$(R-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{R-1}$ imply, with probability at least
$1-\delta_\mu-\delta_\sigma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-source-margin}
 \left|\widehat m-m\right|
 \le r_\mu(\delta_\mu)+z_{1-\alpha}r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma),
 \qquad \sigma\ge10^{-3}.
\end{equation}
The variance floor is harmless here because $a\mapsto\max\{a,10^{-3}\}$ is
one-Lipschitz.  Allocating the two tail probabilities across the finite set of
tasks, profiles, and score heads gives the simultaneous radius required by
Proposition \ref{prop:rank-recovery}.  This result deliberately leaves nearly
tied profiles unresolved: three replications cannot reliably order them.  The
sensitivity experiment therefore varies replications and library size instead
of treating the source order as known.  The normal and chi-square sampling
laws supply the probability bounds \citep{Cochran1934}; Lean checks the finite
variance identity, the effect of the variance floor, the union of bad events,
and the final rank implication.

\subsection{When library coverage reaches a target safe region}

Let $m_q(h)$ be the target chance margin, with $m_q(h)\le0$ implying
feasibility.  Let $z_q^\star$ be an ideal target structural coordinate and
$\widehat z$ the implemented structural projection of $\eta$.  The following
statement exposes the assumption that source and target share useful profile
geometry; it imposes no target interpretation on $(r^g,r^f)$.  Write
$d_q(h,h')=\|z_q^\star(h)-z_q^\star(h')\|_2$.

\begin{assumption}[Aligned profile geometry]\label{ass:alignment}
There exist a target safe-center profile $h_q^\star$, a library member $h_s$,
and constants $\gamma_q,\Delta_q,\epsilon_\eta,L_q\ge0$ such that
\begin{align*}
 m_q(h_q^\star)&\le-\gamma_q,\\
 d_q(h_s,h_q^\star)&\le\Delta_q,\\
 \|\widehat z(h)-z_q^\star(h)\|_2&\le\epsilon_\eta,\\
 |m_q(h)-m_q(h')|&\le L_q d_q(h,h').
\end{align*}
The third inequality holds for every profile used below.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Aligned atlas coverage]\label{thm:aligned-coverage}
Let $\atlas$ cover $\cL$ within radius $r_{n_0}^\eta$ in augmented $\eta$.
By \eqref{eq:projection-cover} it covers the same support within that radius in
$\widehat z$.  Under Assumption \ref{ass:alignment}, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage-condition}
 L_q\bigl(r_{n_0}^\eta+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta\bigr)\le\gamma_q,
\end{equation}
then $\atlas$ contains a target-feasible policy.
\end{theorem}

The condition has a direct reading.  The ten profiles must cover the library
closely; the source-supported shape must not move too far on the target; the
implemented coordinate must approximate the target-relevant geometry; and the
safe region must be deep enough to absorb all three errors.  Frequency shifts,
irregular grids, and misspecification increase $\Delta_q$ or
$\epsilon_\eta$, while a shallow feasible region decreases $\gamma_q$.  In
those cases the theorem stops, as it should.  The complete triangle-inequality
proof appears in the Online Supplement.

Figure \ref{fig:coverage-geometry} gives the geometric interpretation.  The
symbol $\epsilon_\eta$ denotes error between the implemented projected
structural coordinate $\widehat z$ and the ideal target coordinate $z_q^\star$;
it does not transfer the source-rank components $(r^g,r^f)$ to the target.  The
circle labeled $r_{n_0}$ depicts the structural-space upper bound obtained by
projecting the augmented-coordinate cover $r_{n_0}^\eta$.

The preceding theorem concerns one target.  To estimate how often the selected
design succeeds over the declared task population, we next treat a design hit
as a bounded random outcome.  The confirmatory generator uses eight fixed,
equally weighted regimes, not one pooled IID law.  At a fixed resolution $d$,
let regime $s$ have
$n_s$ independent held-out task seeds, weight $w_s$ with $\sum_sw_s=1$, and
hit indicators $H_{si}\in[0,1]$.  Define the task-law hit rate
$p=\sum_s w_s\E[H_s]$, its stratified estimate
$\widehat p=\sum_s(w_s/n_s)\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}H_{si}$, and
$v_w=\sum_s w_s^2/n_s$.  Weighted Hoeffding concentration, which does not
require identical distributions across strata, gives with probability at least
$1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:task-law-bound}
 p\ge \widehat p-\sqrt{\frac{v_w\log(2/\delta)}{2}}.
\end{equation}
Here $w_s=1/8$ and $n_s=20$.  The deterministic component
\texttt{task\_seed mod 5} is an induced latent category whose realized counts
need not be balanced; independence and boundedness, rather than identical
distributions, are sufficient.  The same 160 latent task seeds are crossed
with all three resolutions, so \eqref{eq:task-law-bound} is applied separately
at each $d$; the pooled 480-cell rate is descriptive.  The probability refers
only to a prospective repetition of the registered stratified generator and
requires the atlas to be frozen before held-out tasks.  It is not a guarantee
for arbitrary future domains.

\subsection{Independent terminal safety}

Fix a shortlist before verification.  Candidate $j$ receives $v_j$ fresh iid
Bernoulli indicators $Z_{jr}=1\{Y^g_{jr}\le\tau\}$ and is certified only when
$\sum_rZ_{jr}=v_j$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-success-test}
 p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j,
 \qquad p_0=1-\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $\delta_j=\delta/K_s$ in the primary protocol.

\begin{theorem}[Candidate and familywise validity]\label{thm:verifier}
For any fixed unsafe candidate with true feasibility probability $p_j<p_0$,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{candidate $j$ is certified}\}=p_j^{v_j}
 \le p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j.
\]
For a frozen shortlist, regardless of dependence among the candidate tests,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{an unsafe policy is deployed}\}
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{K_s}\delta_j\le\delta.
\]
Sequentially stopping at the first certificate does not alter this union-bound
argument because the shortlist and all budgets are fixed before testing.
\end{theorem}

For $p_0=.95$, $K_s=3$, and $\delta=.05$, the minimum all-success budget is 80
per candidate.  Its power at true feasibility probability $p$ is $p^{80}$;
this is only 0.017 at $p=.95$.  Validity therefore does not imply that the
test will often certify, and Section \ref{sec:results} reports both validity
and power.  The exact Clopper--Pearson lower bound is used only for the
preregistered sensitivity calculation \citep{ClopperPearson1934}.  A complete
proof of the familywise statement appears in the Online Supplement.

\subsection{Human-readable and machine-checked proofs}

The Online Supplement gives ordinary mathematical proofs of every named result
in this section, the task-law bound, the finite-atlas limitation, and the call
accounting identities.  A bidirectional closure table maps each manuscript
statement to exact Lean declarations in the frozen paper-facing interface.
These declarations build in Lean 4 without \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, or a
project-defined \texttt{axiom}.  Machine checking confirms that the stated
conclusions follow from the stated premises.  It cannot establish that
Assumption \ref{ass:alignment} holds in Energy or in an unspecified future
domain, nor can it replace the declared Gaussian, bounded-task, and binomial
sampling laws; those remain scientific premises.
