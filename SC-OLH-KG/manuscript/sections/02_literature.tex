\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Stochastic and Chance-Constrained Simulation Optimization}

Simulation optimization allocates a finite evaluation budget among noisy
alternatives when analytic objective and constraint functions are unavailable
\citep{Fu2002,AmaranEtAl2016}.  Stochastic kriging makes the simulation noise
explicit and permits heteroscedastic metamodels
\citep{AnkenmanNelsonStaum2010}.  Constrained Bayesian optimization extends
surrogate-guided search to noisy feasibility observations
\citep{LethamEtAl2019}.  Our departure is to make feasible-basin coverage a
separate transferred design problem and to reserve fresh samples for terminal
verification.  The online optimizer can therefore change without changing the
frontend or the deployment certificate.

\subsection{High-Dimensional Bayesian Optimization}

High-dimensional Bayesian optimization relies on structural restrictions.
TuRBO localizes the search in adaptive trust regions
\citep{ErikssonEtAl2019}; SCBO combines trust regions with constrained
posterior sampling \citep{ErikssonPoloczek2021}; and SAASBO imposes sparse
axis-aligned length-scale priors \citep{ErikssonJankowiak2021}.  These methods
are strong target-only backends when enough target evaluations reveal relevant
directions.  They do not, by themselves, ensure that an initial design of ten
points intersects a small chance-feasible basin in a thousand-dimensional
integer cube.  We use canonical SAASBO as the strongest audited backend, not
as a methodological contribution, and compare against target-only TuRBO,
SCBO, and SAASBO under both equal target budget and equal total simulation
cost.

\subsection{Transfer and Meta-Learning for Bayesian Optimization}

Several lines of work transfer source information at different levels.  FSBO
learns a deep-kernel surrogate from source tasks
\citep{WistubaGrabocka2021}.  PACOH and Safe F-PACOH learn function-space
priors with reliability guarantees \citep{RothfussEtAl2021,RothfussEtAl2023}.
HyperBO pretrains Gaussian-process priors from historical functions
\citep{WangEtAl2024HyperBO}.  Transfer GP methods model source-target
discrepancy, while RGPE combines source experts according to target evidence
\citep{TighineanuEtAl2022,FeurerEtAl2018}.  MetaBO and MALIBO transfer query
utilities or acquisition behavior rather than only the surrogate
\citep{VolppEtAl2020,PanEtAl2024}.

Our learning target is different.  We transfer a finite \emph{proposal
measure} over normalized policy shapes before target observations, then expose
the same initial design to multiple backend families.  This makes the
frontend's causal contribution observable.  It also provides a fair
archive-level comparison: all transfer methods receive the same 384-call
source archive, the same target budget, and the same independent terminal
verifier.

\subsection{Structural Priors, Coverage, and Certification}

No-free-lunch results imply that optimization gains require assumptions about
the function class or the distribution of tasks
\citep{WolpertMacready1997}.  Our theory makes that point concrete for finite
target-label-free proposal sets and then states a positive geometric coverage
condition.  The dimension-equivariant coordinate is not claimed to be a
universal representation; it is a low-frequency task hypothesis that can be
audited and falsified.

Final safety introduces a second distinction.  Posterior feasibility used
during search is model dependent, whereas deployment certification should be
auditable independently of the acquisition rule.  We use candidate-wise
fresh-sample bounds and familywise error spending.  For the external
electricity experiment, an exact binomial bridge connects the all-success rule
to the classical one-sided Clopper--Pearson lower confidence bound
\citep{ClopperPearson1934}.  This verification layer is deliberately more
conservative and more expensive than a posterior label; its cost is reported
rather than absorbed into the search budget.
