\section{Additional Implementation Details}\label{app:implementation}

\subsection{Synthetic Population Interfaces}

All synthetic simulators return three Gaussian observations: two objective
components and one constraint component.  The scalar objective is the equal-
weight average of the first two.  The chance constraint uses the population
mean and heteroscedastic standard deviation of the third as in
\eqref{eq:true-margin}.  Each raw coordinate is integer valued between 0 and
100.  The domain summaries are deterministic functions of the normalized
policy and are available to the simulator; analytic target optima and
feasibility labels are unavailable to the algorithm.

FactorShock uses a policy-level summary $(u,q,s)$ and the cumulative exposure
blocks
\[
 A=(u\text{-gap},q\text{-gap},\text{spread}),\qquad
 N=(\text{oscillation},\text{tail shock}).
\]
Inventory partitions the policy into stock, reorder, and safety blocks; Queue
partitions it into capacity, priority, and smoothing blocks.  Each objective
and safety pocket is smooth in its block means and dispersion, but the centers,
loss weights, and variance mechanisms differ by domain.  The post-run oracle
enumerates the low-dimensional summary grid to compute the feasible optimum.

\subsection{Canonical Backend and Discretization}

Canonical SAASBO is implemented in BoTorch with a fully Bayesian single-task
GP and sparse axis-aligned shrinkage prior.  The model is refit after each of
the three adaptive target evaluations.  Proposed normalized policies are
rounded to the target integer cube before simulation.  The source archive is
not appended as 384 pseudo-target observations; its primary role is to train
the atlas.  Transfer baselines use the archive according to their own model
contracts.

\subsection{Terminal Verifier}

The synthetic verifier freezes, deduplicates, and orders up to three policies:
an objective challenger, the backend recommendation, and a safe-interior
support candidate.  Safety is evaluated with fresh Gaussian replications under
a one-sided mean/scale tolerance rule and familywise error spending.  Candidate
budgets are 80, 128, and 128, and the verifier stops at the first certificate.
When a challenger and incumbent are both admissible, each receives eight fresh
objective replications and the challenger switches only when the one-sided
upper bound on its paired cost difference is below zero.  No verifier sample
is returned to the surrogate, acquisition, shortlist construction, or source
model.

\section{Reproducibility and Data Contracts}\label{app:reproducibility}

Each result row records the task fingerprint, source-archive fingerprint,
initial-design fingerprint, method identity, implementation commit, verifier
signature, source/search/verification counts, and terminal truth reconstruction.
The release audit requires every registered cell exactly once, identical
problem fingerprints within paired comparisons, complete target-search traces,
and agreement between stored and reconstructed truth.

Manuscript tables and figures are generated only from compact audited JSON and
CSV artifacts.  The renderer explicitly excludes runtime checkpoints, pickle
files, model weights, and policy vectors, and writes SHA-256 hashes for every
input and output.  The experiment registry also retains named algorithm
variants: periodic-capped SAAS is never relabeled as canonical SAASBO, and
failed or incomplete development tracks are not silently merged into release
results.

The code release includes the final method manifest, compact result records,
paired-statistics artifact, convergence summaries, external energy data
contract, proof receipt, and executable tests.  The OPSD source data are cited
by version and DOI \citep{OPSD2020}.  Runtime checkpoints and model weights are
not required to reproduce the audited numerical tables and are excluded from
the compact release.
