\section{Finite Algorithm}\label{app:algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Algorithm 1: Source-scored structural initial design}
\begingroup
\small
\begin{enumerate}
\item Generate the declared 64-profile outcome-free library on 128 regular
midpoints using seed 20260808.
\item For every source task and profile, compute the replicated objective mean
and the Gaussian chance-margin statistic in \eqref{eq:source-margin}.
\item Convert each statistic to deterministic within-task average-tie
percentile ranks; average the ranks uniformly across source tasks.
\item Compute exact Voronoi-cell cosine coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_8$, divide
$c_k$ by $1+.25k$, append all nine diagonal squares, and standardize the 18
coordinates over the library.
\item Append source safety and objective ranks.  Select the first center by
\eqref{eq:first-center}; select nine further centers by
\eqref{eq:farthest-first}.
\item Linearly interpolate each selected profile to the declared target grid,
clip to bounds, and deterministically round.  Freeze these ten policies before
the first target outcome.
\item Run any declared target backend.  Freeze a distinct ordered shortlist
before verification.
\item Test each shortlist candidate on its independent all-success stream.
Deploy the first certificate; abstain if none certifies.
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup

The stable tie order is safety rank, objective rank, profile identifier for the
first center and finite-library index for farthest-first.  Robust source
feasibility is diagnostic only.  There is no endpoint replacement, hidden
scalarization, target-outcome repair, or target-oracle branch in a non-oracle
arm.

\section{Human-Readable Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

This section gives the complete conventional proof chain used by the paper.
It includes the two short geometric arguments rather than leaving them as
verbal sketches.  Section \ref{app:lean} then maps every step to an exact Lean
declaration and separates proved implications from scientific premises.

\subsection{Profile-coordinate stability}\label{app:proof-profile}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:coefficient-stability}.}
Let $e_d=h-\widehat h_d$.  Since the integration interval has length one,
the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 |c_k(h)-c_k(\widehat h_d)|
 &=\left|\int_0^1 e_d(t)b_k(t)\,dt\right|\\
 &\le\int_0^1 |e_d(t)|\,|b_k(t)|\,dt
 \le\epsilon_dB_k.
\end{align*}
For the regular-midpoint reconstruction, every $t$ lies within $1/(2d)$ of
the midpoint assigned to its Voronoi cell.  Lipschitz continuity therefore
implies
\[
 |h(t)-\widehat h_d(t)|\le L_h/(2d),
\]
so substituting $\epsilon_d=L_h/(2d)$ proves the first rate.  Dividing both
coefficients by $1+\kappa k\ge1$ divides their absolute difference by the same
quantity and cannot enlarge it.

For completeness, suppose $t$ lies between adjacent reference nodes $l$ and
$r$, has interpolation weight $\theta\in[0,1]$, and the node spacing is
$1/d_{\rm ref}$.  The interpolant is
$\widehat h(t)=(1-\theta)h(l)+\theta h(r)$, whence
\begin{align*}
 |h(t)-\widehat h(t)|
 &\le L_h\{(1-\theta)|t-l|+\theta|t-r|\}\\
 &=\frac{2L_h\theta(1-\theta)}{d_{\rm ref}}
 \le\frac{L_h}{2d_{\rm ref}}.
\end{align*}
This is the claimed pre-rounding interpolation bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Farthest-first coverage}\label{app:proof-kcenter}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:farthest-first}.}
Let $r=r(A_k;u)$ and choose a library point $w$ whose distance to $A_k$ is
$r$.  Farthest-first selection implies that the $k$ selected centers together
with $w$ are pairwise separated by at least $r$: when each center was inserted,
its distance from all earlier centers was at least the final covering radius,
and the same is true of $w$ by construction.

Assign these $k+1$ witnesses to their nearest centers in an optimal $k$-center
solution of radius $r_k^\star$.  Two witnesses, say $x$ and $y$, must share one
optimal center $o$.  The triangle inequality then yields
\[
 r\le\|u(x)-u(y)\|_2
 \le\|u(x)-u(o)\|_2+\|u(o)-u(y)\|_2
 \le2r_k^\star.
\]
The zero-radius case is immediate.  Finally, deleting the two source-rank
coordinates is a Euclidean projection and is nonexpansive.  Hence an
$\eta=(z,r^g,r^f)$ cover of radius $r$ is also a $z$ cover of radius at most
$r$, proving Equation \eqref{eq:projection-cover}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite source errors and separated-rank recovery}
\label{app:proof-rank}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rank-recovery} and the
finite-source margin bound.}
Fix a source task and a library profile.  For its $R$ independent Gaussian
constraint replications, write $\bar Y$ and $S$ for the sample mean and sample
standard deviation, and write $\mu$ and $\sigma$ for their population
counterparts.  The Gaussian mean tail gives
\[
 \Prob\{ |\bar Y-\mu|>r_\mu(\delta_\mu)\}
 \le \delta_\mu.
\]
Let $\nu=R-1$.  Cochran's identity gives
$(R-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_\nu$, and therefore, with probability at least
$1-\delta_\sigma$,
\[
 \sqrt{q_{\nu,\delta_\sigma/2}/\nu}
 \le \frac{S}{\sigma}
 \le \sqrt{q_{\nu,1-\delta_\sigma/2}/\nu}.
\]
On this event,
\[
 |S-\sigma|
 \le \sigma\max\{a_\nu(\delta_\sigma),
                         b_\nu(\delta_\sigma)\}
 =r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma).
\]
Because $\sigma\ge10^{-3}$, replacing $S$ by
$\max\{S,10^{-3}\}$ cannot increase its distance from $\sigma$.  Hence, on
the intersection of the mean and scale events,
\begin{align*}
 |\widehat m-m|
 &=\left| (\bar Y-\mu)
       +z_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\max\{S,10^{-3}\}-\sigma\bigr)\right|\\
 &\le r_\mu(\delta_\mu)
      +z_{1-\alpha}r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma).
\end{align*}
The objective score uses the same argument without the scale term.  Assigning
the tail probabilities across the finite set of source tasks, profiles, and
score heads and applying the union bound produces a simultaneous radius $r_q$
for each score head.

It remains to show what this event implies for ranking.  Let profiles $j$ and
$\ell$ have true scores $s_{qj}<s_{q\ell}$ with
$s_{q\ell}-s_{qj}>2r_q$.  Uniform error at most $r_q$ gives
\[
 \widehat s_{qj}
 \le s_{qj}+r_q
 < s_{q\ell}-r_q
 \le \widehat s_{q\ell}.
\]
Thus every pair separated by more than $2r_q$ has the correct estimated order.
Pairs inside that band may remain tied or reverse, which is why the result
does not claim exact recovery of the full percentile ranking.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From structural coverage to a feasible target policy}
\label{app:proof-aligned}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:aligned-coverage}.}
By Assumption \ref{ass:alignment}, choose a source-supported library profile
$h_s$ satisfying
$d_q(h_s,h_q^\star)\le\Delta_q$.  Because the selected atlas covers the
library in augmented coordinate $\eta$, there is an atlas member $a$ such that
\[
 \|\eta(a)-\eta(h_s)\|_2\le r_{n_0}^\eta.
\]
Projection onto the structural coordinates is nonexpansive, so
\[
 \|\widehat z(a)-\widehat z(h_s)\|_2
 \le r_{n_0}^\eta.
\]
Insert $\widehat z(a)$, $\widehat z(h_s)$, and
$z_q^\star(h_s)$ between the ideal target coordinates of $a$ and the safe
center.  The triangle inequality and the two coordinate-approximation bounds
give
\begin{align*}
 d_q(a,h_q^\star)
 &\le \|z_q^\star(a)-\widehat z(a)\|_2
      +\|\widehat z(a)-\widehat z(h_s)\|_2\\
 &\quad
      +\|\widehat z(h_s)-z_q^\star(h_s)\|_2
      +d_q(h_s,h_q^\star)\\
 &\le r_{n_0}^\eta+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta.
\end{align*}
The one-sided Lipschitz condition for the chance margin now yields
\begin{align*}
 m_q(a)
 &\le m_q(h_q^\star)+L_qd_q(a,h_q^\star)\\
 &\le -\gamma_q
      +L_q(r_{n_0}^\eta+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta)
 \le0,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality is exactly condition
\eqref{eq:coverage-condition}.  Therefore $a$ is target feasible and belongs
to the selected atlas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stratified task-law concentration}\label{app:proof-task-law}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Equation \eqref{eq:task-law-bound}.}
Index the independent held-out task outcomes by $(s,i)$ and write
$a_{si}=w_s/n_s$.  Then
\[
 \widehat p-p
 =\sum_s\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{si}
   \{H_{si}-\E[H_{si}]\}.
\]
The variables need not have the same distribution.  Independence and
$H_{si}\in[0,1]$ are enough for Hoeffding's lemma, which gives
\[
 \E\exp\{\lambda(\widehat p-p)\}
 \le \exp\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{8}
        \sum_s\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{si}^2\right\}
 =\exp\{\lambda^2v_w/8\},
\]
because $\sum_i a_{si}^2=w_s^2/n_s$.  Chernoff optimization at
$\lambda=4r/v_w$ gives
\[
 \Prob\{\widehat p-p\ge r\}\le e^{-2r^2/v_w}.
\]
Applying the same argument to $p-\widehat p$ and taking a union bound yields
$\Prob\{|\widehat p-p|\ge r\}\le2e^{-2r^2/v_w}$.  Set
\[
 r=\sqrt{\frac{v_w\log(2/\delta)}{2}}.
\]
The right-hand side is $\delta$, and on the complementary event
$p\ge\widehat p-r$, which is Equation \eqref{eq:task-law-bound}.  Applying the
argument separately at each resolution is valid because independence is
required within a resolution, not between the three reported resolutions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact familywise verification}\label{app:proof-verifier}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:verifier}.}
Consider a fixed candidate $j$ whose true success probability satisfies
$p_j<p_0$.  Certification requires all $v_j$ fresh Bernoulli trials to
succeed.  Independence within that candidate's verification stream gives
\[
 \Prob\{j\text{ certifies}\}=p_j^{v_j}
 \le p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j.
\]
If an unsafe policy is deployed from the fixed shortlist, at least one unsafe
shortlist member must have certified.  Consequently,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{an unsafe policy is deployed}\}
 \le \sum_{j:\,p_j<p_0}\Prob\{j\text{ certifies}\}
 \le \sum_{j=1}^{K_s}\delta_j
 \le\delta.
\]
This union bound does not require independence between candidates.  Testing
them in a fixed order and stopping at the first certificate only removes later
tests from consideration; it does not enlarge the event that any unsafe
candidate passes.  Fixing the shortlist, its order, and all budgets before
verification is what prevents search or selection from using the fresh test
outcomes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-only scope, no-free-lunch, and call accounting}
\label{app:proof-scope}

The frozen proposal is a function of source records and the declared target
grid only.  Replacing one unused target-label object by another therefore
leaves the proposal unchanged.  This is a definitional noninterference result,
not a probabilistic generalization claim.

For the corresponding limitation, let $\mathcal X$ be finite and let a
deterministic target-label-free atlas $A$ be a proper subset of $\mathcal X$.
Choose $x\in\mathcal X\setminus A$ and define the nonempty feasible set as
$\mathcal F=\{x\}$.  Then $A\cap\mathcal F=\varnothing$.  In particular,
$|A|\le n_0<|\mathcal X|$ implies that such a feasible set exists.  Thus a
finite source-only proposal needs the alignment premise in Theorem
\ref{thm:aligned-coverage}; coverage of every possible target is impossible.

Finally, if the source archive costs $S$ simulations, target search costs $N$,
and independent verification costs $V$, disjoint accounting gives
$C_{\rm all}=S+N+V$.  If the same frozen archive is reused by $M$ target
problems, charging each target an equal share of the archive gives
$C_{\rm amort}(M)=S/M+N+V$.  These are accounting identities; they do not claim
that archive reuse is statistically valid for an unrelated target.

matrices and 12 compact analyses.  It retains the one recorded algorithmic
failure.  Every row identifies the task, source archive, initial design,
