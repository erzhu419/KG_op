\section{Finite Algorithm}\label{app:algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Algorithm 1: Source-scored structural initial design}
\begingroup
\small
\begin{enumerate}
\item Generate the declared 64-profile outcome-free library on 128 regular
midpoints using library seed 20261799, equal to family seed 20260808 plus 991.
\item For every source task and profile, compute the replicated objective mean
and the Gaussian chance-margin statistic in \eqref{eq:source-margin}.
\item Convert each statistic to deterministic within-task average-tie
percentile ranks; average the ranks uniformly across source tasks.
\item Compute exact Voronoi-cell cosine coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_8$, divide
$c_k$ by $1+.25k$, append all nine diagonal squares, and standardize the 18
coordinates over the library.
\item Append source safety and objective ranks.  Select the first center by
\eqref{eq:first-center}; select nine further centers by
\eqref{eq:farthest-first}.
\item Linearly interpolate each selected profile to the declared target grid,
clip to bounds, and deterministically round.  Freeze these ten policies before
the first target outcome.
\item Run any declared target backend.  Freeze a distinct ordered shortlist
before verification.
\item Test each shortlist candidate on its independent all-success stream.
Deploy the first certificate; abstain if none certifies.
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup

The stable tie order is safety rank, objective rank, profile identifier for the
first center and finite-library index for farthest-first.  Robust source
feasibility is diagnostic only.  There is no endpoint replacement, hidden
scalarization, target-outcome repair, or target-oracle branch in a non-oracle
arm.

\subsection{Supplementary comparison rules}\label{app:supplemental-controls}

Standardized DCT maximin computes the same 18-dimensional $z_j$ as the
source-scored atlas.  It starts at the library medoid minimizing mean
Euclidean distance to all 64 profiles and adds nine farthest-first centers in
$z$; the lowest library index breaks ties.  The original unstandardized DCT
maximin control applies these rules directly to $\phi$.

The source-greedy portfolio uses the same replicated source records and
within-task percentile ranks as the source-scored atlas.  Define
$\rho_{qj}=.5(r^g_{qj}+r^f_{qj})$.  Starting from $A_0=\varnothing$, it selects
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greedy-portfolio}
 j_\ell\in\argmin_{j\notin A_{\ell-1}}
 \frac1m\sum_{q=1}^{m}\min_{i\in A_{\ell-1}\cup\{j\}}\rho_{qi},
 \qquad A_\ell=A_{\ell-1}\cup\{j_\ell\},\quad \ell=1,\ldots,10.
\end{equation}
Ties favor the candidate with the lower overall mean source score
$m^{-1}\sum_q\rho_{qj}$, then the lower library index.  This adapts greedy
portfolio selection \citep{FeurerEtAl2022} to the declared finite policy
library using safety and objective ranks.
Both supplementary designs use the original interpolation, target budget,
and independent verifier.  Their evaluations reuse the original target seeds
after manuscript review.

\section{Human-Readable Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:design-stability}.}
A normalized average-tie rank is $(J-1)^{-1}$ times the sum, over all other
profiles, of one for a lower score and one-half for an equal score.  Under the
simultaneous error event, a pair separated by more than $2r_q$ keeps its strict
order.  Only the $U_{qj}(r_q)$ unresolved pairs can change their contribution,
each by at most one.  This proves the rank bound.  Weighted averages propagate
these bounds to $\epsilon_g$ and $\epsilon_f$.

Since $|\widehat b_j-b_j|\le\epsilon_b$ and
$\widehat b_{\widehat j}\le\widehat b_{j^\star}$ for a population minimizer,
$b_{\widehat j}\le b_{j^\star}+2\epsilon_b$.  A population gap strictly
larger than $2\epsilon_b$ forces the same unique minimizer.  Structural
coordinates are fixed, so each augmented coordinate moves by at most
$\epsilon_{\rm aug}$ and each pairwise distance by at most
$2\epsilon_{\rm aug}$.
For a common selected set, taking the minimum over its members preserves this
distance-error bound.  A winner-to-runner-up gap larger than
$4\epsilon_{\rm aug}$ therefore preserves the next center.  Induction from the
common first center proves equality of the complete sequence.
\Halmos
\end{proof}

\subsection{First-center uncertainty and target safety}

For a simultaneous source-score bound
$\max_j|\widehat b_j-b_j|\le\epsilon_b$, define
\[
 \mathcal C_b=\{j:\widehat b_j\le\min_\ell\widehat b_\ell+2\epsilon_b\},
 \qquad D_b=\max_{i,j\in\mathcal C_b}
 \|\widehat z(h_i)-\widehat z(h_j)\|_2.
\]

\begin{proposition}[First-center margin transfer]\label{prop:first-center-transfer}
Let $j^\star$ minimize $b_j$ and $\widehat j$ minimize $\widehat b_j$.
On the simultaneous score-error event, both belong to $\mathcal C_b$.
Suppose the target margin is $L_q$-Lipschitz in $z_q^\star$, coordinate
error is at most $\epsilon_\eta$ on this set, and
$m_q(h_{j^\star})\le-\gamma_q$.  Then
\[
 m_q(h_{\widehat j})\le-\gamma_q+L_q(D_b+2\epsilon_\eta).
\]
Thus $L_q(D_b+2\epsilon_\eta)\le\gamma_q$ suffices for a feasible first
center.  If the score-error event has probability at least $1-\delta_s$
and the target premises hold throughout that event, the same lower bound
applies to the probability of a feasible first center.
\end{proposition}

The Online Supplement gives the proof.  Unlike complete-design stability,
this result permits the first center to change within a geometrically small
source-compatible set.  It separates source estimation error from target
transfer.  Source data alone cannot identify whether the selected center is
safer than a distinct outcome-free center: the same archive can accompany
two targets assigning opposite safety margins to those policies.

\paragraph{Scope of the first-center condition.}
For a supplied valid source radius, $\mathcal C_b$ and $D_b$ use only
source scores and fixed coordinates.  The target safety of $j^\star$,
$L_q$, and coordinate mismatch remain target premises.  The source radii
above depend on population scales and unresolved population-score pairs;
substituting sample scales does not automatically yield a computable valid
confidence radius.  We do not use this analytic result to select policies
or certify deployment in the experiments.
A postdecision diagnostic on the 240 confirmation tasks evaluates the
condition using exact source-score errors and a finite-library target
Lipschitz constant.  It satisfies the sufficient condition on 0/240 tasks;
the source-compatible sets contain 14--33 policies.  Thus the condition
clarifies the logical connection but does not explain the observed
certification gain with a nonvacuous safety bound.

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:first-center-transfer}.}
Let $k$ minimize $\widehat b$.  On the source-score error event,
\[
 \widehat b_{j^\star}\le b_{j^\star}+\epsilon_b
 \le b_k+\epsilon_b\le\widehat b_k+2\epsilon_b.
\]
Hence $j^\star\in\mathcal C_b$; $\widehat j\in\mathcal C_b$ by definition.
The coordinate triangle inequality gives
\[
 \|z_q^\star(h_{\widehat j})-z_q^\star(h_{j^\star})\|_2
 \le D_b+2\epsilon_\eta.
\]
Apply target Lipschitz continuity and the margin bound at $j^\star$.
A nonpositive margin implies feasibility.  If all target premises hold
throughout the score-error event, that event is contained in the
feasible-first-center event, proving the probability claim.
\Halmos
\end{proof}

For first-center nonidentifiability, fix a source archive and two distinct
policies $a,c$.  On target 1 set deterministic margins
$m_1(a)=-1$, $m_1(c)=1$; on target 2 set $m_2(a)=1$, $m_2(c)=-1$.
All remaining library margins are $1$ on both targets.  Source outcomes,
coordinates and source-only selections are unchanged, but which policy is
safe reverses.  Source records cannot distinguish these targets without an
additional target restriction.  This construction establishes a scope
limit; it does not assert that either target occurs in the experiments.

This section gives the complete conventional proof chain used by the paper.
It includes the two short geometric arguments rather than leaving them as
verbal sketches.  Section \ref{app:lean} then maps the original proof chain to exact Lean
declarations and separates proved implications from scientific premises.

\subsection{Profile-coordinate stability}\label{app:proof-profile}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:coefficient-stability}.}
Let $e_d=h-\widehat h_d$.  Since the integration interval has length one,
the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 |c_k(h)-c_k(\widehat h_d)|
 &=\left|\int_0^1 e_d(t)b_k(t)\,dt\right|\\
 &\le\int_0^1 |e_d(t)|\,|b_k(t)|\,dt
 \le\epsilon_dB_k.
\end{align*}
For the regular-midpoint reconstruction, every $t$ lies within $1/(2d)$ of
the midpoint assigned to its Voronoi cell.  Lipschitz continuity therefore
implies
\[
 |h(t)-\widehat h_d(t)|\le L_h/(2d),
\]
so substituting $\epsilon_d=L_h/(2d)$ proves the first rate.  Dividing both
coefficients by $1+\kappa k\ge1$ divides their absolute difference by the same
quantity and cannot enlarge it.

For completeness, suppose $t$ lies between adjacent reference nodes $l$ and
$r$, has interpolation weight $\theta\in[0,1]$, and the node spacing is
$1/d_{\rm ref}$.  The interpolant is
$\widehat h(t)=(1-\theta)h(l)+\theta h(r)$, whence
\begin{align*}
 |h(t)-\widehat h(t)|
 &\le L_h\{(1-\theta)|t-l|+\theta|t-r|\}\\
 &=\frac{2L_h\theta(1-\theta)}{d_{\rm ref}}
 \le\frac{L_h}{2d_{\rm ref}}.
\end{align*}
This is the claimed pre-rounding interpolation bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Farthest-first coverage}\label{app:proof-kcenter}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:farthest-first}.}
Let $r=r(A_k;u)$ and choose a library point $w$ whose distance to $A_k$ is
$r$.  Farthest-first selection implies that the $k$ selected centers together
with $w$ are pairwise separated by at least $r$: when each center was inserted,
its distance from all earlier centers was at least the final covering radius,
and the same is true of $w$ by construction.

Assign these $k+1$ witnesses to their nearest centers in an optimal $k$-center
solution of radius $r_k^\star$.  Two witnesses, say $x$ and $y$, must share one
optimal center $o$.  The triangle inequality then yields
\[
 r\le\|u(x)-u(y)\|_2
 \le\|u(x)-u(o)\|_2+\|u(o)-u(y)\|_2
 \le2r_k^\star.
\]
The zero-radius case is immediate.  Finally, deleting the two source-rank
coordinates is a Euclidean projection and is nonexpansive.  Hence an
$\eta=(z,r^g,r^f)$ cover of radius $r$ is also a $z$ cover of radius at most
$r$, proving Equation \eqref{eq:projection-cover}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite source errors and separated-rank recovery}
\label{app:proof-rank}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rank-recovery} and the
finite-source margin bound.}
Fix a source task and a library profile.  For its $R$ independent Gaussian
constraint replications, write $\bar Y$ and $S$ for the sample mean and sample
standard deviation, and write $\mu$ and $\sigma$ for their population
counterparts.  The Gaussian mean tail gives
\[
 \Prob\{ |\bar Y-\mu|>r_\mu(\delta_\mu)\}
 \le \delta_\mu.
\]
Let $\nu=R-1$.  Cochran's identity gives
$(R-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_\nu$, and therefore, with probability at least
$1-\delta_\sigma$,
\[
 \sqrt{q_{\nu,\delta_\sigma/2}/\nu}
 \le \frac{S}{\sigma}
 \le \sqrt{q_{\nu,1-\delta_\sigma/2}/\nu}.
\]
On this event,
\[
 |S-\sigma|
 \le \sigma\max\{a_\nu(\delta_\sigma),
                         b_\nu(\delta_\sigma)\}
 =r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma).
\]
Because $\sigma\ge10^{-3}$, replacing $S$ by
$\max\{S,10^{-3}\}$ cannot increase its distance from $\sigma$.  Hence, on
the intersection of the mean and scale events,
\begin{align*}
 |\widehat m-m|
 &=\left| (\bar Y-\mu)
       +z_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\max\{S,10^{-3}\}-\sigma\bigr)\right|\\
 &\le r_\mu(\delta_\mu)
      +z_{1-\alpha}r_\sigma(\delta_\sigma).
\end{align*}
The objective score uses the same argument without the scale term.  Assigning
the tail probabilities across the finite set of source tasks, profiles, and
score heads and applying the union bound produces a simultaneous radius $r_q$
for each score head.

It remains to show what this event implies for ranking.  Let profiles $j$ and
$\ell$ have true scores $s_{qj}<s_{q\ell}$ with
$s_{q\ell}-s_{qj}>2r_q$.  Uniform error at most $r_q$ gives
\[
 \widehat s_{qj}
 \le s_{qj}+r_q
 < s_{q\ell}-r_q
 \le \widehat s_{q\ell}.
\]
Thus every pair separated by more than $2r_q$ has the correct estimated order.
Pairs inside that band may remain tied or reverse, which is why the result
does not claim exact recovery of the full percentile ranking.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From structural coverage to a feasible target policy}
\label{app:proof-aligned}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:aligned-coverage}.}
By Assumption \ref{ass:alignment}, choose a source-supported library profile
$h_s$ satisfying
$d_q(h_s,h_q^\star)\le\Delta_q$.  Because the selected atlas covers the
library in augmented coordinate $\eta$, there is an atlas member $a$ such that
\[
 \|\eta(a)-\eta(h_s)\|_2\le r_{n_0}^\eta.
\]
Projection onto the structural coordinates is nonexpansive, so
\[
 \|\widehat z(a)-\widehat z(h_s)\|_2
 \le r_{n_0}^\eta.
\]
Insert $\widehat z(a)$, $\widehat z(h_s)$, and
$z_q^\star(h_s)$ between the ideal target coordinates of $a$ and the safe
center.  The triangle inequality and the two coordinate-approximation bounds
give
\begin{align*}
 d_q(a,h_q^\star)
 &\le \|z_q^\star(a)-\widehat z(a)\|_2
      +\|\widehat z(a)-\widehat z(h_s)\|_2\\
 &\quad
      +\|\widehat z(h_s)-z_q^\star(h_s)\|_2
      +d_q(h_s,h_q^\star)\\
 &\le r_{n_0}^\eta+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta.
\end{align*}
The one-sided Lipschitz condition for the chance margin now yields
\begin{align*}
 m_q(a)
 &\le m_q(h_q^\star)+L_qd_q(a,h_q^\star)\\
 &\le -\gamma_q
      +L_q(r_{n_0}^\eta+\Delta_q+2\epsilon_\eta)
 \le0,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality is exactly condition
\eqref{eq:coverage-condition}.  Therefore $a$ is target feasible and belongs
to the selected atlas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stratified task-law concentration}\label{app:proof-task-law}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Equation \eqref{eq:task-law-bound}.}
Index the independent held-out task outcomes by $(s,i)$ and write
$a_{si}=w_s/n_s$.  Then
\[
 \widehat p-p
 =\sum_s\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{si}
   \{H_{si}-\E[H_{si}]\}.
\]
The variables need not have the same distribution.  Independence and
$H_{si}\in[0,1]$ are enough for Hoeffding's lemma, which gives
\[
 \E\exp\{\lambda(\widehat p-p)\}
 \le \exp\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{8}
        \sum_s\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{si}^2\right\}
 =\exp\{\lambda^2v_w/8\},
\]
because $\sum_i a_{si}^2=w_s^2/n_s$.  Chernoff optimization at
$\lambda=4r/v_w$ gives
\[
 \Prob\{\widehat p-p\ge r\}\le e^{-2r^2/v_w}.
\]
Applying the same argument to $p-\widehat p$ and taking a union bound yields
$\Prob\{|\widehat p-p|\ge r\}\le2e^{-2r^2/v_w}$.  Set
\[
 r=\sqrt{\frac{v_w\log(2/\delta)}{2}}.
\]
The right-hand side is $\delta$, and on the complementary event
$p\ge\widehat p-r$, which is Equation \eqref{eq:task-law-bound}.  Applying the
argument separately at each resolution is valid because independence is
required within a resolution, not between the three reported resolutions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact familywise verification}\label{app:proof-verifier}

\begin{proof}{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:verifier}.}
Consider a fixed candidate $j$ whose true success probability satisfies
$p_j<p_0$.  Certification requires all $v_j$ fresh Bernoulli trials to
succeed.  Independence within that candidate's verification stream gives
\[
 \Prob\{j\text{ certifies}\}=p_j^{v_j}
 \le p_0^{v_j}\le\delta_j.
\]
If an unsafe policy is deployed from the fixed shortlist, at least one unsafe
shortlist member must have certified.  Consequently,
\[
 \Prob\{\text{an unsafe policy is deployed}\}
 \le \sum_{j:\,p_j<p_0}\Prob\{j\text{ certifies}\}
 \le \sum_{j=1}^{K_s}\delta_j
 \le\delta.
\]
This union bound does not require independence between candidates.  Testing
them in a fixed order and stopping at the first certificate only removes later
tests from consideration; it does not enlarge the event that any unsafe
candidate passes.  Fixing the shortlist, its order, and all budgets before
verification is what prevents search or selection from using the fresh test
outcomes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-only scope, no-free-lunch, and call accounting}
\label{app:proof-scope}

The frozen proposal is a function of source records and the declared target
grid only.  Replacing one unused target-label object by another therefore
leaves the proposal unchanged.  This is a definitional noninterference result,
not a probabilistic generalization claim.

For the corresponding limitation, let $\mathcal X$ be finite and let a
deterministic target-label-free atlas $A$ be a proper subset of $\mathcal X$.
Choose $x\in\mathcal X\setminus A$ and define the nonempty feasible set as
$\mathcal F=\{x\}$.  Then $A\cap\mathcal F=\varnothing$.  In particular,
$|A|\le n_0<|\mathcal X|$ implies that such a feasible set exists.  Thus a
finite source-only proposal needs the alignment premise in Theorem
\ref{thm:aligned-coverage}; coverage of every possible target is impossible.

Finally, if the source archive costs $S$ simulations, target search costs $N$,
and independent verification costs $V$, disjoint accounting gives
$C_{\rm all}=S+N+V$.  If the same frozen archive is reused by $M$ target
problems, charging each target an equal share of the archive gives
$C_{\rm amort}(M)=S/M+N+V$.  These are accounting identities; they do not claim
that archive reuse is statistically valid for an unrelated target.

The map distinguishes logical closure from empirical truth.  Assumption

distributional inputs; Lean verifies the finite-sample variance identity,
event union, variance-floor propagation, and rank implication after those
inputs are supplied.  Likewise, the task-law result assumes independent

binomial law for each fresh stream.  These boundaries are part of the theorem
statements rather than unrecorded gaps.

\section{Public-battery development replay}\label{ec:public-battery}

This diagnostic uses the original 154 joint policy profiles, of which 100
are constant pairs, and three target seasons beginning April 15, July 15,
and October 15, 2024.  Each season has 73 supported window starts, from 0 to
4320 at stride 60.  The original controller and failure rule are fixed.
The source archive contains 44,968 evaluated cells from two 2023 seasons,
154 profiles, 73 windows and two failure rules.  The rules give identical
outcomes and successful costs here.  The source-selected center is
\texttt{constant\_pair\_0007\_0007}, with channel levels .75/.75.

Each search evaluates 13 distinct candidates on one uniformly sampled window

three additional policies follow a common frozen random permutation.

only the source center and retains this conservative allocation.

\section{First-center margin diagnostic}

After all confirmation decisions were fixed, we reconstructed the same
24 source archives using their original seeds and replayed 9,216 source
replications.  Every estimated first center matched its stored decision.
No new independent source samples or target simulations were collected.
For the original 240 confirmation tasks we evaluated generator truth on
all 64 mapped library policies (15,360 deterministic evaluations).
Population objective and chance-margin ranks define $b_j$ with the original
equal weights and tie breaks.  The diagnostic radius is the observed
$\max_j|\widehat b_j-b_j|$, not a confidence radius.

For this finite-domain check choose $z_q^\star=\widehat z$, so coordinate
error is zero, and compute the exact maximum of
$|m_q(h_i)-m_q(h_j)|/\|\widehat z(h_i)-\widehat z(h_j)\|_2$
over distinct coordinates in the mapped library.  No coordinate collision
with unequal margins occurred.  This supplies a Lipschitz constant only on
that finite library, not on the continuous policy space.

The sufficient condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:first-center-transfer}
holds on 0/240 tasks.  Source-compatible sets have 14--33 policies.
Population-score first centers are target feasible in 119/240 tasks;
estimated first centers are feasible in 106/240.  These totals do not imply
that removing source noise improves every task: for example, piecewise
smoothness has 14/30 and 22/30 respectively.  The proposed bound is therefore
empirically vacuous in this diagnostic.  It remains a conditional analytic
statement, not an established explanation of the experimental gain or a
selection rule.  Compact results and code are retained under
\texttt{first\_center\_margin\_diagnostic\_20261002} in the current
submission attachment.
