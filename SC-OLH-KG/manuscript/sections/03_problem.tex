\section{Problem and Information Contract}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Held-Out Chance-Constrained Task}

Let $q$ index a simulation task and let
$\cX_{q,d}=\{0,1,\ldots,L_q\}^{d}$ be its finite integer policy space.  A
simulation at $\x\in\cX_{q,d}$ returns a noisy objective $F_q(\x)$ and a noisy
constraint response $G_q(\x)$.  The target task is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-problem}
 \min_{\x\in\cX_{q,d}} f_q(\x):=\E[F_q(\x)]
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \Prob\{G_q(\x)\le 0\}\ge 1-\alpha .
\end{equation}
For the Gaussian synthetic environments, the equivalent population chance
margin is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:true-margin}
 m_q(\x)=\mu_{G,q}(\x)+z_{1-\alpha}\sigma_{G,q}(\x),
 \qquad \x\text{ is feasible iff }m_q(\x)\le 0,
\end{equation}
where $z_{1-\alpha}$ is the standard normal quantile.  The experiments use
$\alpha=0.05$.  Two simulated objective outputs are scalarized with fixed
weights $(1/2,1/2)$; neither the weights nor the chance threshold is tuned by
target seed.

The known synthetic oracle is used only after each run to score true
feasibility and feasible regret,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret}
 r_q(\widehat\x)=f_q(\widehat\x)-
 \min_{\x:m_q(\x)\le0}f_q(\x),
\end{equation}
and never for proposal construction, target search, or verification.  Regret
is undefined when the recommendation is truly infeasible, so feasibility
counts and conditional regret are always reported together.

\subsection{Source, Search, and Verification Information}

Suppose $Q_s$ source tasks have been completed before target task $q$ is
opened.  Their archive is
\begin{equation}
 \cD_{\mathrm{src}}=
 \{(q',\x_{q'i},Y^F_{q'ir},Y^G_{q'ir}):q'\ne q\},
\end{equation}
with observed simulation outputs but no source optimizer, source feasible
oracle, or target label.  The final leave-one-domain-out protocol uses two
source domains, 64 shared normalized profiles per source domain, and three
independent replications per profile, for $S=384$ source simulator calls.

Before observing a target response, a deterministic training map produces an
atlas of at most $n_0$ target policies,
\begin{equation}
 \atlas=T(\cD_{\mathrm{src}},d,L_q,\mathsf{schema}_q),
 \qquad |\atlas|\le n_0.
\end{equation}
The target schema may identify dimension, integer bounds, and unlabeled policy
or state channels.  It may not contain target objective values, constraint
values, feasible labels, target optimizers, or terminal verification outcomes.
The atlas is immutable once the target stage begins.

The target optimizer first evaluates $n_0$ atlas points and may then request
$N-n_0$ adaptive target evaluations.  In the primary experiment $n_0=10$ and
$N=13$.  After search, it freezes an ordered shortlist $O_N$ and terminates.
An independent verifier draws $V$ additional target samples.  These samples do
not update the optimizer or the shortlist.  Thus the exact simulation cost is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 C_{\mathrm{total}}=S+N+V,
\end{equation}
and not $N$.  We report source calls, target-search calls, objective-comparison
calls, safety-verification calls, and total calls as separate fields.

\subsection{Three-Part Decision Protocol}

The protocol has three interfaces.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item The \emph{frontend} maps source observations and target descriptors to
the frozen atlas $\atlas$.
\item The \emph{backend} maps atlas and adaptive target observations to a
shortlist.  It may be proposal-only, a transfer GP, SC-V69, or canonical
SAASBO.
\item The \emph{verifier} maps the frozen shortlist and fresh samples to a
deployment recommendation or no certificate.
\end{enumerate}

This factorization is central to both theory and experiment.  Atlas coverage
does not depend on the backend.  The verifier's error bound does not depend on
either the atlas or the backend.  Conversely, success under a strong initial
design cannot automatically be attributed to the online acquisition rule.
