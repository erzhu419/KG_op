\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Many simulation-optimization decisions are functions before they are vectors.
A reserve rule varies with forecast stress, an inventory rule varies across a
planning horizon, and a resource rule varies over ordered operating states.
Implementation discretizes such a policy into hundreds or thousands of
coordinates, but neighboring coordinates retain a common order and often a
smooth or piecewise-smooth interpretation.  Treating this representation as an
arbitrary high-dimensional box discards information that can matter more than
the choice of sequential optimizer.

The difficulty is sharper under chance constraints.  With only a few target
simulations, an unstructured design may never observe a feasible policy.  A
surrogate or acquisition function then has little evidence with which to learn
the feasible boundary.  Historical simulations from related tasks appear to
offer a remedy, but they introduce two risks.  First, source tasks can be
misaligned: a shape that is safe in one system may be harmful in another.
Second, source cost and final safety verification can dwarf the advertised
target-search budget.  A credible method must therefore expose its structural
assumption, separate source information from target information, and permit
abstention when independent evidence is insufficient.

We study a deliberately narrow question: can replicated source outcomes
improve the \emph{initial design} for a new chance-constrained ordered-profile
problem?  We begin with a finite, outcome-free library of constant, monotone,
low-frequency, piecewise, and high-frequency profiles on $[0,1]$.  Source
outcomes do not create this library.  They score its members using within-task
objective and chance-margin ranks.  A first profile balances the two ranks;
Gonzalez farthest-first selection then fills the design in a coordinate that
contains exact cosine coefficients, their diagonal squares, and the two source
ranks.  The ten selected profiles are frozen and mapped to the target grid
before any target outcome is observed.  We call the result a
\emph{source-scored structural initial design}.

This construction separates three decisions that are often conflated.  The
frontend decides where target search starts.  A replaceable backend may add
target evaluations.  A frozen ordered shortlist is then tested on fresh
replications by an optimizer-independent verifier.  Figure
\ref{fig:method-overview} shows the information flow.  Verification outcomes
never return to search.

Our contributions are fourfold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item We give a complete finite algorithm for turning source simulations into
an initial design for ordered profiles.  Every library rule, coordinate,
weight, tie break, interpolation, and information boundary is explicit.  The
method uses no target objective, feasibility label, optimum, safe-basin
parameter, or verification outcome before target search.

\item We connect the implementation to a finite theoretical chain.  The
cosine coordinate is stable under profile-grid refinement; farthest-first is a
two-approximation for finite $k$-center coverage; bounded source estimation
error preserves sufficiently separated ranks; and profile coverage implies
target feasibility under explicit source-target discrepancy and safety-depth
conditions.  A separate exact-binomial result controls familywise false
deployment for the frozen shortlist.  The finite implications are
machine-checked in Lean 4; the alignment assumptions remain empirical scope
conditions rather than universal claims.

\item We conduct a frozen stress test designed to reveal benchmark fit.  Eight
randomized regimes vary effective rank, frequency support, coordinate order,
grid regularity, smoothness, sparse high-frequency activity, and complete
source-target misspecification.  Structured source-free controls use the same
finite library and profile coordinate.  Additional matrices vary source
budget, representation schema, chance level, safety mass, and method constants.

\item We account for source, target-search, and verification calls separately.
An equal preverification-cost experiment gives target-only methods the source
archive's calls.  A region-held-out electricity study is retained even though
it reverses the source-versus-generic ordering.  Native transfer methods use
their own pipelines rather than receiving our initial design.  These negative
and equal-cost results delimit the contribution: source scoring is useful
under aligned task laws and repeated deployment, not as a universal shortcut
for arbitrary high-dimensional optimization.
\end{enumerate}

The main empirical result is correspondingly conditional.  Across 480
independent randomized tasks per design, source scoring substantially improves
aggregate feasible coverage and certification.  It loses, however, in several
registered regimes and in the electricity study.  A $10{,}000$-coordinate
result is therefore interpreted as refinement of an ordered profile grid, not
as solving an arbitrary $10{,}000$-variable black-box problem.  This boundary
is central to both the theory and the practical decision of whether historical
simulation is worth buying.
