\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A policy stored as thousands of ordered numbers may still be one simple rule.
We use source simulations to rank transparent policy shapes, farthest-first
selection to keep ten starting policies diverse, and fresh simulations to make
the final safety decision.  Matched comparisons and prospective new-family
confirmation support a certification advantage over standardized generic
selection.  Factorial controls identify the source-selected first center as
the main effective component, while the structural metric adds a smaller
increment over raw profile distance.  A later fixed-first-center diagnostic
does not establish a certification advantage over nine random remainder
policies.  The prospective portfolio comparison
favors the portfolio in feasible coverage and penalized loss; an outcome-free
design has lower calls per certificate in Energy, and additional target search wins at equal
single-target preverification cost.  The public-battery development diagnostic is matched by a source-free
constant-policy search and does not repay source queries.  The supported
contribution is therefore source-selected initialization under specified
synthetic task laws, with finite conditional guarantees and explicit
verification.  Archive reuse in an operational domain requires a separate
comparison against simple policies and source-inclusive target budgets.
