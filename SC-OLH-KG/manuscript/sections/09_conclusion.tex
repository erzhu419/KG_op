\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}

We developed a source-scored structural initial design for
chance-constrained ordered policy profiles.  Replicated source outcomes rank a
public finite profile library; a cross-grid cosine coordinate and
farthest-first rule select ten target policies; a replaceable backend performs
search; and an independent verifier either certifies a frozen candidate or
abstains.  The method's finite implementation is linked to profile-consistency,
covering, rank-recovery, conditional transfer, and exact verification results.

The confirmatory evidence is informative because it is mixed.  Source scoring
substantially improves aggregate coverage and certification over generic
structure in a registered randomized task law.  It fails under specific
high-frequency, irregular, and misspecified regimes, loses to generic
structure in a region-held-out Energy study, and is not best when source calls
are reassigned to one target.  Historical simulation is therefore useful as
an amortized, task-law-dependent design investment---not as a universal cure
for high dimensionality.  The broader lesson is that, for expensive profile
decisions, making structural and information assumptions explicit can be more
valuable than adding another optimization backend.
