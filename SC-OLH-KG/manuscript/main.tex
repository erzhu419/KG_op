\RUNTITLE{Structural Initial Designs for Ordered Policy Profiles}
\TITLE{Source-Scored Structural Initial Designs for
Chance-Constrained Optimization of Ordered Policy Profiles}
\ARTICLEAUTHORS{\AUTHOR{Authors omitted for peer review}}

\ABSTRACT{Many policies stored as thousands of ordered settings are finely
discretized rules. We study how historical simulations can choose starting
policies for a new chance-constrained problem of this kind. Replicated source
outcomes rank a public library of 64 profiles by objective and safety;
farthest-first selection chooses ten profiles covering a standardized cosine
coordinate augmented by these ranks. The design is fixed before target
outcomes and followed by an independent deployment verifier. We establish
cross-grid stability, finite-library coverage, recovery of separated source
ranks, conditional coverage under source-target alignment, and familywise
certificate validity. Across the original 480 task--resolution cells,
source scoring achieves 47.3\% certified true-feasible deployment versus
31.5\% for standardized outcome-free maximin. A prospective study on 240 new
tasks, three new source families and three public libraries yields 23.3\%
versus 10.0\%. Factorial controls attribute most of the gain to the
source-selected first center; rank augmentation adds little. A subsequent
fixed-first-center diagnostic gives 53/240 certificates with nine random
remainder policies versus 54/240 with structural coverage. A same-source
portfolio gives better feasible coverage and penalized loss in the new study,
while electricity results do not establish an archive cost advantage.
A separate public-battery development replay is matched by a source-free
constant-policy search and favors paid enumeration at the source-inclusive budget.
The evidence supports source-selected initialization under the declared
synthetic task laws; its operational value requires transferable structure
and recovery of the archive cost.}
