Chance-Constrained Optimization of Ordered Policy Profiles}}
\author{Authors omitted for review}

\begin{abstract}
We study expensive chance-constrained simulation optimization for ordered
policy profiles with historical source tasks.  Before any target outcome, our
method ranks a public 64-profile library from replicated source objective and
chance-margin outcomes, then chooses ten profiles by farthest-first coverage
in an exact cosine-profile coordinate.  Target search and independent
verification remain replaceable, so the contribution is an initial design,
not a new Bayesian-optimization backend.  We establish cross-grid coordinate
consistency, a factor-two finite-library covering guarantee, source-rank
recovery, conditional source-to-target coverage, and exact familywise safety
for a frozen shortlist.  In a registered randomized task law with eight
aligned and adverse regimes, 160 independently seeded latent tasks crossed
with three grid resolutions (480 task--resolution cells per design),
the source-scored design finds a truly feasible policy in 91.7\% of tasks and
an independently certified one in 47.3\%, versus 37.5\% and 10.6\% for an
outcome-free cosine maximin design.  The comparison reverses in aligned,
irregular-grid, sparse-high-frequency, and misspecified regimes.  In a
region-held-out electricity-reserve study, source scoring certifies 60/90 runs
versus 70/90 for the generic design; target-only functional optimization also
wins at equal one-target preverification cost.  Historical simulation is
therefore justified only as an amortized, task-law-dependent design
investment.
\end{abstract}
