\RUNTITLE{Source-Learned Structural Priors for Chance Constraints}
\TITLE{Source-Learned Structural Priors for Chance-Constrained Simulation Optimization}
\ARTICLEAUTHORS{\AUTHOR{Authors omitted for peer review}}
\ABSTRACT{We study chance-constrained simulation optimization when a policy is
represented by an ordered profile and related source tasks are available before
the target opens.  A finite public function dictionary learns objective,
constraint, and cumulative-risk structure from replicated source simulations.
Paid target observations update the target function posteriors and the
physical-risk family weights, while a correlated verification-aware
knowledge-gradient rule allocates the remaining budget.  On a declared development contract,
observable trajectory exposures improve early mean prediction, and a paired
source-structure control has higher mean final reward than a same-source full
dictionary.  The evidence is bounded: only three of eight development tasks
improve, an independently frozen eight-task block does not replicate the mean
ordering, a generic target-only RBF-KG control is at least as strong on both
blocks, and learned risk has no independent deployment gain.  We therefore
claim a source-learned structural prior within the declared representation and
dictionary as a development-block effect, rather than a stable deployment
advantage, learned trajectory embeddings, factor-level risk recovery, or
general high-dimensional optimization.}
\KEYWORDS{simulation optimization, transfer learning, structured priors, chance constraints, knowledge gradient}
