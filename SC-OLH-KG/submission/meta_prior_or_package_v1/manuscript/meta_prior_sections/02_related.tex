\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:mp_related}

Simulation optimization studies expensive stochastic systems whose objective or
constraints are available only through simulation \citep{Fu2002,AmaranEtAl2016}.
Stochastic kriging separates response uncertainty from simulation noise
\citep{AnkenmanNelsonStaum2010}, while constrained Bayesian optimization models
noisy feasibility observations jointly \citep{LethamEtAl2019}.  We study the
additional transfer question: how should a finite source archive influence the
target posterior before the target has many observations?

Transfer Bayesian optimization includes learned initializations
\citep{WistubaEtAl2015,FeurerEtAl2022}, hierarchical and deep-kernel priors
\citep{WistubaGrabocka2021,RothfussEtAl2021,RothfussEtAl2021FPACOH}, and
multi-task hyperparameter models such as HyperBO
\citep{WangEtAl2024HyperBO}.  Our transfer object is narrower.  We learn a
finite function prior from replicated source responses, then update that prior
with paid target observations.  The method does not retrieve related tasks
from an unrestricted repository and does not reuse a source optimum as a
target label.

Chance-constrained ranking and selection separates search from a final
independent decision \citep{HongLuoNelson2015,BoeselNelsonKim2003}.  We retain
that separation: the posterior ranks candidates, while a fresh verifier makes
the deployment decision.  The resulting claim is conditional on a declared
ordered-profile representation and source-target task law; no distribution-free
optimizer is claimed \citep{WolpertMacready1997}.
