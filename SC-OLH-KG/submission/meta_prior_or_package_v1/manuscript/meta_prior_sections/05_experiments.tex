\section{Experimental Contracts}\label{sec:mp_experiments}

\subsection{Source archive and target protocol}

The frozen M1 archive contains eight source tasks, 64 public policies per
task, and 12 independent repetitions per policy (6,144 source episodes).  The
target uses the same public candidate bank, eight common initial policies, 24
search points, and 928 independent verification replications.  All arms share
initial points, target seeds, keyed observation streams, and the verification
procedure.

The primary comparisons separate four questions: (i) prediction after the
common initial design, (ii) target correction of a mismatched source structure,
(iii) risk calibration, and (iv) final verified decision utility.  Source,
target, search, and verification calls are accounted for separately.

\subsection{Feasible-deployment development contract}

The original Inventory law often produced no feasible policy in the 96-point
bank.  A separate development contract therefore fixes the public constraint
price range to $[0.4,0.9]$, while retaining the demand schedule, capacity,
budget, candidate bank, and verifier.  Its eight development law seeds have
19--75 feasible candidates and feasible-objective spans of 0.053--0.130 under
the oracle screen.  This screen is a task-contract preflight; it is not
available to the learner.  The original confirmation interval 95433--95440
was not run as a block, but seed 95434 overlaps the development artifact and
is therefore excluded from any clean-confirmation claim.  A separate v2
contract freezes eight new law seeds (95800--95807),
records an oracle-only preflight before target observations, retains every
seed, and reports the resulting independent block without reading the
original block.

The final attribution control compares source-selected structure with the
same-source full dictionary under identical observable coordinates and pooled
risk.  It uses the same eight tasks and the same keyed target stream.  This is
development evidence, not evidence that the source-structure effect
generalizes to an independent block.

\subsection{Negative and external controls}

We retain the original adverse Inventory law, the physical-risk factor-family
diagnostics, the official HyperBO interface and its no-feasible-target loop,
and the legacy atlas/Energy studies as separate evidence identities.  These
controls are not pooled into a single performance table because their source
archives, initial designs, risk heads, or verification contracts differ.
