# Cross-family contract preflight

The strong source-prior claim cannot enter a new comparison until the target contracts expose a meaningful decision problem. The outcome-free screens now show four different situations.

* **Inventory:** the feasible `[0.4,0.9]` contract is usable for paired deployment, but its independent v2 block is negative for source-selected structure. It remains adverse evidence.
* **Energy:** the full 64-profile library has zero audit-feasible candidates for DK_1 under the current OPSD 2018, 1000-hour, `alpha=0.05` reliability rule. Average constraint means are negative, but the window-level reliability condition rejects every candidate. This contract cannot support a decision ranking.
* **Queue:** the fixed 64-point bank has 6--9 feasible candidates, but their feasible objective span is only `0.0028--0.0229`; a fixed 5x5x5 constant grid gives `0.0017--0.0224`. Both are below the frozen `0.04` separation gate. The contract has feasible points but insufficient decision separation.
* **FactorShock:** a registered structured bank passes the feasibility and span screen for some fixed geometry tasks but fails it for others. It is a possible replacement family only after a new task and candidate contract is frozen.

The consequence is operational: no mature cross-family deployment matrix should be launched yet. Reusing Energy or Queue by silently relaxing reliability or separation would change the estimand after seeing the preflight. The next legitimate work is to write new family contracts with a deterministic, scale-aware separation rule and a complete three-head observation interface, then rerun only the outcome-free preflight. If those contracts do not pass, the paper cannot support stable or universal source-prior decision superiority.
