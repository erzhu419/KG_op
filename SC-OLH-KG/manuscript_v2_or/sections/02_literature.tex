\section{Literature Positioning}\label{sec:lit}

Our framework connects four streams of research across operations research and statistical machine learning.

\paragraph{Knowledge Gradient and Heteroscedastic Simulation Metamodeling.}
The Knowledge Gradient (KG) policy maximizes the expected single-step value of information under correlated normal beliefs \citep{frazier2008knowledge,frazier2009correlated} and continuous GP surrogates \citep{scott2011correlated}. For stochastic simulations with input-dependent noise, Stochastic Kriging \citep{ankenman2010stochastic} and heteroscedastic GPs \citep[\texttt{hetGP};][]{binois2018practical,binois2019replication} separate extrinsic surface uncertainty from intrinsic Monte Carlo variance and allocate budget between exploring new policies and replicating evaluated ones; \citet{makarova2021risk} incorporate input-dependent variance into risk-averse BO. GP-FTA adds cross-task transfer and models trajectory variance using nonnegative state-risk exposures $(A_{\mathrm{local}}(x),N_{\mathrm{shared}}(x))$ fitted to within-policy dispersion $S^2(x)/(r-1)$. GP-FTA-KG uses a one-step lookahead proxy, while GP-FTA-TS uses joint posterior sampling. Both link their search scores to the conditional power of a prospective exact binomial verification gate.

\citet{ungredda2024bayesian} use posterior expected reward weighted by the probability of feasibility for their terminal recommendation, then value an observation by its expected improvement in that recommendation. GP-FTA adopts this decision-focused idea for a different terminal event. Its weight is the posterior probability of passing a specified finite-sample verification rule, which averages the binomial test's operating characteristic over the posterior violation rate. For example, a policy exactly at the 5\% business risk limit passes the registered first test with probability $0.95^{112}+112(0.05)0.95^{111}\simeq 0.0221$. This makes the validation budget relevant to search even when policies are feasible. The implemented score uses a first-slot proxy. Section~\ref{sec:cascade_value} gives the full-rule value and conditions for agreement in query ranking.

\paragraph{High-Dimensional and Trajectory-Summary Bayesian Optimization.}
Cold-start high-dimensional BO relies on local trust regions \citep[\texttt{TuRBO} and \texttt{SCBO};][]{eriksson2019scalable,eriksson2021scalable}, hierarchical sparse axis-aligned priors \citep[\texttt{SAASBO};][]{eriksson2021high}, or additive functional kernels \citep{kandasamy2015high,lu2022additive}. In dynamic policy search, \citet{wilson2014using} show that kernels over state-action trajectories capture behavioral similarity missed by parameter-space distance. Because mapping $x \in [0,1]^{d_t}$ onto a fixed-dimensional nominal rollout summary $\psi(x) \in \R^{\PsiDim}$ is necessarily many-to-one ($d_t \gg \PsiDim$), we neither treat $\psi$ as an injective coordinate chart nor discard raw policy structure: smooth multi-channel DCT templates preserve temporal/spatial phase ordering in candidate generation, while a nonparametric residual GP over $\psi$ absorbs task-specific nonlinearities beyond the low-rank source basis. To compare methods with common public features, we also benchmark \texttt{SCBO} and \texttt{RGPE} on $\psi(x)$ (\texttt{SCBO-on-Psi} and \texttt{RGPE-on-Psi}). These methods use their own candidate proposal rules.

\paragraph{Meta-Learning BO, Empirical-Bayes Shrinkage, and Misspecified Prior Tempering.}
Meta-BO methods transfer information across tasks via leave-one-out ranking ensembles \citep[\texttt{RGPE};][]{feurer2018scalable}, empirical-Bayes multi-task GP pre-training \citep[\texttt{HyperBO};][]{wang2024pretrained}, or PAC-Bayesian hyperpriors \citep[\texttt{PACOH};][]{rothfuss2021pacoh}. Operational legacy archives differ from standard meta-BO benchmarks in four respects: source and target dimensions differ ($d_s \neq d_t$), adaptive optimizers heavily oversample elite basins \citep{spall1992multivariate,hansen2006cma,deb2002fast}, enterprise ``dark-data'' logs combine single-replication ($r_a=1$) runs with unequal task budgets and mismatched risk tolerances ($\alpha_m \neq \alpha_t$), and misspecified linear/hierarchical priors suffer variance inflation or asymptotic drag unless tempered and released \citep{bissiri2016general,grunwald2017inconsistency,rasmussen2006gaussian}. Classical Stein--Mallows unbiased risk estimation \citep{mallows1973some,efron1973stein,stein1981estimation} gives a scalar comparison for adaptive tempering. Theorems~\ref{thm:transfer_boundary} and~\ref{thm:sure_uniform} describe the MSE boundary and conditional excess risk in that model. The implemented GP uses plug-in leave-one-out predictive scores.

\paragraph{Chance-Constrained Simulation Optimization and Cascade Clean-Up.}
Simulation-based approaches to chance-constrained optimization include Monte Carlo approximations and constrained Bayesian optimization \citep{hong2011sequential,letham2019constrained}. Reusing noisy search evaluations to certify the selected policy can introduce selection bias. Chance-constrained ranking and selection controls feasibility and selection on a specified finite set under explicit sampling and indifference-zone conditions \citep{hong2015chance}. Following the separation of search and final selection in simulation clean-up \citep{boesel2003using}, GP-FTA freezes a short search-selected roster and uses fresh exact binomial gates \citep{clopper1934use}. The conditional joint error bound controls unsafe deployment when the fresh verification blocks follow the deployment risk law. The ranking of certified points depends on the implemented guard and carries no guaranteed objective superiority.
