\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

GP-FTA transfers information across policy dimensions through public summaries of nominal system behavior. The finite coefficient prior defines which responses at different policies move together. With fixed initialization and common proposal and verification rules, changing only the off-diagonal coefficients of its covariance changes most primary and operating policies in both families of the matched panel. The change produces improvements and losses within each family. The mean loss effects point in opposite directions in queueing and traffic, with wide paired intervals. Deletion of one of six source tasks frequently reverses those policy comparisons, including after common target initialization. It is useful to check the in-archive ordering before relying on a specific estimated association. The early predictive rule shows no detectable loss benefit. Its target is the constraint forecast, which is only one component of deployment value.

Finite-sample gate power and posterior feasibility give different lookahead weights. On the matched panel the mean loss favors feasibility weighting in both families, and the cost difference in terminal calls depends on the family. On saved traffic paths, adding the later slots reverses the ordering of the mean operating losses for those weights. The guarded full-sequence value and interval screen provide an alternative decision rule for query selection. They make explicit which branch probabilities and objective comparisons determine whether the first-slot approximation can be used.

For a new task, the deletion intervals can flag unsupported policy orderings before deployment. A cautious use of historical coupling would defer a choice whose ordering changes when one source is dropped. The data here do not estimate the operating loss of a policy that uses that screen. For the query score, there is a model-conditional test. The first-slot choice suffices when its lower full-rule value exceeds all other upper values, or when the gap bound falls within the operator's loss tolerance. Full-rule integration can focus on the remaining contenders.

Independent terminal verification controls the probability of a certified unsafe return under its sampling law. Safety of the policy actually operated also depends on the fallback. All traffic anchors on both prospective panels exceed the threshold in independent audits. The operational comparison therefore includes them in the safety and loss denominators. Earlier raw-space comparisons and archive-amortization estimates describe their saved panels and budgets. The independent comparisons give evidence for terminal-call savings in inventory, while the matched panels show how benefits of transfer and of the lookahead weight depend on the task family and comparator.
