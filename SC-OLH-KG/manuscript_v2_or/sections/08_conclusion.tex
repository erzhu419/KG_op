\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

GP-FTA transfers information across policy dimensions through public summaries of nominal system behavior. The finite coefficient prior defines which responses at different policies move together. With fixed initialization and common proposal and verification rules, changing only the off-diagonal coefficients of its covariance changes most primary and operating policies in both families of the matched panel. The change produces improvements and losses within each family. The mean loss effects point in opposite directions in queueing and traffic, with wide paired intervals. The prospective early rule based on the initial predictive score seldom releases the historical prior and shows no detectable loss benefit.

Finite-sample gate power and posterior feasibility give different lookahead weights. On the matched panel the mean loss favors feasibility weighting in both families, and the cost difference in terminal calls depends on the family. The guarded full-sequence value and its ranking condition specify when information from the first-slot score is enough to order queries. They also identify the additional branch probabilities and objective observations needed to evaluate the full terminal decision.

Independent terminal verification controls the probability of a certified unsafe return under its sampling law. Safety of the policy actually operated also depends on the fallback. All traffic anchors on both prospective panels exceed the threshold in independent audits. The operational comparison therefore includes them in the safety and loss denominators. Earlier raw-space comparisons and archive-amortization estimates describe their saved panels and budgets. The independent comparisons give evidence for terminal-call savings in inventory, while the matched panels show how benefits of transfer and of the lookahead weight depend on the task family and comparator.
