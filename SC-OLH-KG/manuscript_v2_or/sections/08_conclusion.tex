\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

When only evaluated policies can enter a finite-sample clean-up test, a search query has two consequences. It updates beliefs about the current shortlist and may make a new policy eligible for verification. Accounting for both in a one-step working-model score improved pooled decision loss relative to chance-feasibility KG. The estimated pooled differences against single-slot KG and the same-representation target-only sampler remained uncertain. Independent verification bounds false certification under the specified verification law, while an abstention leaves the operator exposed to the status quo. This distinction matters in traffic, where the status quo violated the emissions threshold.

After eight inventory observations, both source priors ranked held-out policies better on average than the raw target GP. The full-minus-raw mean change in rank correlation was $+0.085$, with a task-paired $95\%$ interval spanning zero ($[-0.038,0.209]$). The prespecified same-basis comparison leaves uncertainty about the benefit for final deployment. The complete warm configuration improved queueing loss relative to the cold source configuration, with initial designs, subsequent proposal generation, and source variance information all changing together. Distinguishing their individual contributions calls for comparisons that vary one component at a time.

In the 336-dimensional storage study, operating risks differed across two disjoint sets of historical blocks from the same period. A temporally ordered evaluation would address prospective risk under later market conditions. For the current decision, the strongest evidence concerns the value of certification-aware search and the conditions under which historical information helps predict policy performance.
