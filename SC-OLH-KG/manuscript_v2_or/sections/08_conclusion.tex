\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

When terminal verification can certify only policies simulated during search and evaluates a guarded multi-slot shortlist, a sequential query alters both posterior beliefs over current contenders and candidate eligibility. Modeling both effects via the shortlist-menu \textbf{EC-KG} proxy significantly reduced standardized deployment loss relative to marginal chance-feasibility KG across the $72$-task DES panel (Holm $p=0.039$) and outperformed sampled-incumbent \texttt{cEI} \citep{ungredda2024bayesian} in $22/24$ frozen production states, at roughly three times the per-step surrogate compute cost of single-slot lookahead. Multi-scramble reference diagnostics also pinpoint the proxy's primary approximation limits: Stage~A marginal screening misses late-stage queueing winners that act through backup slots, and retaining the pre-query shortlist in the menu proxy can overvalue high-variance candidates relative to unconditional single nomination on individual states. Independent binomial clean-up guarantees false-certification control under the verification law, whereas abstention retains status-quo risk when legacy plans are non-compliant (as in urban signal retiming) and interleaved historical block shifts can still produce localized market exceedances (concentrated in two of fourteen European grid zones).

Separating representation engineering, offline functional priors, and warm-starting clarifies how historical solver traces aid a new planning study. Pre-registered single-probe confirmation on $72$ unseen tasks demonstrates genuine kernel-mediated cross-policy propagation on constraint surfaces across all three DES domains and on traffic objectives, and shows that with $M_{\mathrm{src}}=6$ source tasks this propagation operates through learned per-mode variances on the spectral basis rather than off-diagonal basis-coefficient covariances—while also documenting negative pure-kernel objective transfer in queueing and scarce-sample negative transfer on grid before online \texttt{SURE} shrinks the prior. In closed-loop decisions, the complete warm system delivers clear gains in queueing (Holm $p=0.002$), whereas pure cold-start closed-loop prior contrasts remain modest and inconclusive at current task counts (Holm $p \ge 0.055$), motivating future work on temporally ordered out-of-sample grid certification and single-factor warm-start designs.
