\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied how certification-aligned search and cross-horizon spectral priors interact in chance-constrained simulation optimization under an independent clean-up cascade over evaluated policies. Aligning lookahead with the guarded three-slot contract via \textbf{EC-KG} lowers pooled standardized loss across $72$ DES tasks relative to marginal chance-feasibility KG (Holm $p=0.039$, driven primarily by finite-sample gate-power alignment) and four implemented search comparators (all Holm $p\le 0.039$), while sample-level curtailment saves $3.1\%$--$33.0\%$ of verification calls ($6.9\%$--$34.1\%$ live streaming wall-clock time) with identical decisions. Pure single-factor counterfactuals on frozen \textbf{GP-FTA-ECKG} states detect no significant pooled loss difference from relaxing evaluated-only eligibility ($p=0.142$) or using unthresholded triple utility maximization ($p=0.878$), and multi-scramble diagnostics delineate both gains over constrained EI variants and approximation losses from Stage~A screening and menu-floor retention.

Mapping variable-dimension policies onto public operational summaries enables offline spectral mode learning with shift-invariant within-policy variance estimation and online Stein--Mallows shrinkage. Across $72$ unseen tasks, learned per-mode variances improve post-probe constraint shape accuracy (Holm $p<0.001$) and unbiased gate-power MSE over target-only GPs (Holm $p=0.042$), whereas off-diagonal covariances remain neutral at $M_{\mathrm{src}}=6$ and unconfirmed under exact-$\omega_M$ scaling at $M_{\mathrm{src}}=12$ ($p=0.119$), nominal rollouts substantially beat forecast-fed generic summaries, and cold-start closed-loop prior contrasts remain inconclusive (Holm $p\ge 0.055$) even as full warm transfer improves queueing decisions (Holm $p=0.002$). Independent clean-up protects verification-law safety while isolating market-clustered grid volatility, motivating regime-adaptive certification and learned cross-task representations.
