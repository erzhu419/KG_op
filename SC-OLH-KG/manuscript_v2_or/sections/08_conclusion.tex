\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

The roster that can be nominated after a query changes with its observations. Comparing scores on that roster aligns the search objective with the next terminal decision. The first-slot score and the full-rule score often agree under this eligibility requirement in the tested initial histories. The bound provides two ways to act before simulating again. A sole retained query is optimal for the specified working model and finite forecast. More generally, an upper bound on the value lost by retaining the first-slot choice can be compared with an operator's tolerance. Any remaining joint integration can focus on surviving queries. In our implementation the repeated model updates and nomination consume considerably more time than the final joint integrations after screening.

Terminal decisions can still have a substantial effect on observed operation. In traffic, adding backup slots reverses the sample ordering of the mean operating losses of the two observed knowledge-gradient methods on their realized search paths. That comparison concerns the deployment rule applied after search. The one-step comparison instead holds model and nomination fixed within each fantasy. Neither the audit risk nor the expected value of later slots alone determines whether the next query changes. Independent verification controls the probability of returning an unsafe certified policy under its sampling law. The safety of the policy actually operated also depends on the fallback.

Legacy data support a common response representation across policy dimensions. In the registered comparisons, historical covariance changes many actual policy choices. The fitted cost orderings remain sensitive to source deletion with both full and diagonal covariance, and the changes attributable to non-diagonal terms fail to survive all deletions. The results support reporting the deletion sensitivity of a policy comparison. Using it to select a prior on another source archive remains an empirical question.
