\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated how certification-aligned sequential search and cross-horizon spectral priors interact in chance-constrained simulation optimization when deployment requires passing an independent finite-sample clean-up cascade over evaluated policies. First, aligning search lookahead with the three-slot guarded clean-up cascade via \textbf{EC-KG}—decomposing working-model value of information into shortlist refinement and candidate admission—lowers standardized deployment loss across $72$ inventory, queueing, and traffic tasks relative to marginal chance-feasibility knowledge gradient (Holm $p=0.039$) and relative to four external frontier baselines spanning trust-region constrained Bayesian optimization, rank-weighted Gaussian process ensembles, warm-started multi-task knowledge gradient, and joint-draw latent noisy expected improvement (all four Holm $p\le 0.039$), closing $61.7\%$--$107.6\%$ of achievable feasible-oracle gaps. Sample-level sequential binomial curtailment preserves the exact full-sample certification indicator on every sample path while cutting verification replications by $14.2\%$--$33.0\%$ across domains with active boundary screening. Controlled counterfactuals confirm that relaxing evaluated-only terminal eligibility yields no loss reduction despite frequent unvisited nominations, that a parameter-free combinatorial shortlist maximizer matches our structured three-role cascade ($p=0.424$), and that frozen-state multi-scramble diagnostics pinpoint both where \textbf{EC-KG} outperforms plug-in and joint-draw constrained expected improvement ($13$W/$9$T/$2$L and $11$W/$9$T/$4$L) and where marginal Stage~A contender screening or menu-proxy floor retention lose single-nomination value.

Second, mapping variable-dimension policies onto deterministic twelve-coordinate public operational summaries enables offline spectral prior learning with shift-invariant within-policy heteroscedastic variance calibration and online Stein--Mallows subspace shrinkage. Pre-registered confirmation across $72$ unseen tasks verifies both cumulative post-probe constraint shape accuracy beyond isotropic and mode-permuted controls (Holm $p<0.001$) and improved finite-sample gate-power calibration over target-only Gaussian processes ($p=0.010$). At $M_{\mathrm{src}}=6$ source tasks, the most reproducible kernel gains arise from learned per-mode variances, while incremental off-diagonal covariance effects do not replicate reliably in pure-kernel tests (turning modestly positive only when scaling to $M_{\mathrm{src}}=12$), public nominal rollouts substantially outperform simulator-free black-box summaries when nominal dynamics are available while remaining resilient to $15\%$ forecast noise, and pure cold-start closed-loop prior contrasts remain statistically inconclusive after multiplicity adjustment (Holm $p\ge 0.055$), whereas full warm transfer delivers significant end-to-end gains in queueing (Holm $p=0.002$). Finally, independent binomial clean-up controls false certification under the verification law while separating abstention fallback risk on non-compliant status-quo plans from market-clustered temporal volatility on European electricity grids, pointing toward regime-adaptive temporal certification and larger-archive closed-loop designs as natural directions for future research.
